What documents do I need to apply for a job in Malta?

A clear checklist for candidates preparing a Malta job application, from your CV and ID to work eligibility, certificates, references, and the details employers may ask for after an offer.

Published July 7, 2026 Candidate advice 5 min read
In this guide Quick answer Core documents Work status Role-specific documents After an offer What not to send early FAQs

Quick answer

To apply for a job in Malta, you usually need an updated CV, accurate contact details, proof of identity, and any role-specific certificates or licences. Employers may later ask for references, work eligibility documents, a residence or work permit, tax or social security details, and bank information after an offer.

If you are applying for jobs in Malta, the most useful thing you can do is prepare your documents before you start sending applications. It helps recruiters move faster, reduces back-and-forth, and makes you look organised from the first message.

That said, not every document belongs in the first application. Some documents are normal to share with a recruiter early, while others should only be shared when an employer genuinely needs them. This guide separates both stages so you can apply confidently and protect your personal information.

The core documents to prepare first

1. An updated CV

Your CV is the main document for most applications in Malta. Keep it focused on your recent experience, practical skills, languages, availability, and the type of role you want. For hospitality, retail, customer support, administration, finance, IT, construction, and technical roles, recruiters need to see what you have done, where you worked, and whether your experience matches the vacancy.

If your CV is not ready yet, start with our guide to CV writing tips for getting hired in Malta.

2. A short application message

You do not always need a formal cover letter, but a short message can help. Mention the role you are applying for, your relevant experience, your current location, your availability, and whether you already have permission to work in Malta if that is relevant.

3. Contact details that actually work

Use a professional email address and a phone number where recruiters can reach you. If you are applying from outside Malta, include your country code and state whether you are available for video interviews.

4. Copies of certificates that match the role

Do not attach every certificate you have ever received. Attach or prepare the ones that matter for the vacancy. For example, a driver should prepare a driving licence; a technician may need trade certificates; a chef may need culinary or food-related documents; a finance candidate may need accounting qualifications.

Documents that depend on your nationality or work status

Work eligibility is one of the biggest reasons candidates get delayed. The documents you may need depend on your nationality, current residence status, and the employer's requirements.

If you are Maltese or already resident in Malta

Prepare your Maltese ID or residence card details, your CV, and any job-specific certificates. You normally do not need to send ID at the first application stage unless the recruiter asks for it for a clear reason.

If you are an EU, EEA, or Swiss candidate

Prepare your passport or national ID, CV, certificates, and a clear note about whether you are already in Malta or planning to relocate. Employers may ask about your availability, accommodation plans, and start date. Requirements can vary by role, so confirm the latest process with the recruiter or official Maltese sources.

If you are a non-EU candidate

Prepare your CV, passport details, current residence or permit status if you have one, and any relevant certificates. If you are not already authorised to work in Malta, do not assume every employer can sponsor or support an application. Ask early and be honest about your status.

Important: This article is general job-search guidance, not legal advice. Work permit, residence, tax, and employment registration rules can change. Candidates should verify the latest requirements with official Maltese authorities or the employer before submitting sensitive documents.

Role-specific documents employers may request

Some jobs in Malta require extra documents because of the type of work, the employer's industry, or regulatory requirements. These are not always needed for the first application, but you should know what may come next.

  • Driving and delivery roles: driving licence, licence category, clean driving history if requested, and sometimes proof of local driving eligibility.
  • Hospitality and food roles: food handling or hygiene certificates where required, previous employer references, and proof of experience.
  • Healthcare and care roles: professional registration, qualifications, references, and role-specific compliance documents.
  • Finance and accounting roles: qualifications, membership details, references, and sometimes police conduct or background checks.
  • Construction and technical roles: trade certificates, safety training, licences, portfolio photos, and equipment-related qualifications.
  • IT and digital roles: portfolio, GitHub or project links, certifications, and examples of systems or tools used.

Documents usually requested after a job offer

Some documents should normally come after you are shortlisted or offered the job, not before. These may include:

  • Bank account or IBAN for payroll
  • Tax details or tax identification information
  • Social security details
  • Signed employment forms
  • Emergency contact details
  • Proof of address if required
  • Police conduct certificate if relevant to the role
  • Medical or fitness documents where the role requires them

If a company asks for sensitive documents before you have had a proper interview or clear job offer, slow down and check whether the request is legitimate.

What not to send too early

For a normal first application, avoid sending full passport scans, bank details, tax details, social security numbers, residence card scans, or personal documents unrelated to the job. A recruiter may eventually need to verify details, but a first application should usually start with your CV and role-relevant information.

FAQs

What documents do I need to apply for a job in Malta?

Most candidates need an updated CV, contact details, proof of identity when requested, and role-specific certificates or licences. Work eligibility, references, tax details, social security details, and bank information are usually requested later, depending on the role and hiring stage.

Do I need a work permit before applying for jobs in Malta?

It depends on your nationality and current residence status. Maltese and EU candidates usually follow a different route from non-EU candidates. Non-EU candidates should be ready to discuss current authorisation status and should verify current requirements with official Maltese sources.

Should I send my passport with every job application?

No. For a first application, your CV is usually enough unless the recruiter specifically asks for proof of identity or eligibility. Share sensitive documents only through trusted channels and only when there is a clear reason.

Do employers in Malta ask for references?

Many employers ask for references after shortlisting or before making a final offer. Prepare referee names and contact details in advance, but ask each referee for permission before sharing their information.

What documents do hospitality candidates need in Malta?

Hospitality candidates should prepare a CV, references, proof of experience, and any food handling, hygiene, language, or role-specific certificates requested by the employer. Requirements vary by role and workplace.
