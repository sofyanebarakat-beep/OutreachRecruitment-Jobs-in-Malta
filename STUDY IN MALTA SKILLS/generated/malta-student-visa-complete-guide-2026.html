1. What Is a Malta Student Visa?

Definition

A Malta student visa is an official authorisation issued by the Maltese government that allows non-EU nationals to enter Malta for the purpose of pursuing academic studies, vocational training, or accredited language courses at an approved educational institution.

Malta is a member of the European Union and the Schengen Area. This means its visa framework follows both EU regulations and the Schengen Agreement, giving students who obtain a Malta student visa access to all 27 Schengen zone countries for short stays during their studies.

There are two distinct phases to the Malta student visa process:

  1. The entry visa — obtained before travelling to Malta, from a Maltese embassy or consulate in your home country.
  2. The Student Residence Permit — obtained after arriving in Malta, from Identità (formerly Identity Malta), the government agency responsible for residence documentation.

Malta and the European Union

Malta joined the European Union in 2004 and the Schengen Area in 2007. A degree obtained in Malta carries full EU recognition, meaning it is valid throughout Europe and widely recognised globally. This makes Malta particularly attractive for students from countries like India, Nigeria, Libya, Tunisia, Egypt, and other non-EU nations seeking a European qualification.

Are You From a Visa-Exempt Country?

Citizens of many countries — including the United States, Canada, Australia, Japan, and others — can enter Malta without a visa for short stays. However, for courses longer than 90 days, even visa-exempt nationals must apply for a Student Residence Permit once inside Malta.

Visa-Required Countries

If you are a national of a country that requires a Schengen visa to enter the EU — including most African, Middle Eastern, and South/Southeast Asian nations — you must apply for a visa before travelling. This is done at the nearest Maltese embassy or consulate, or at an embassy representing Malta if no Maltese diplomatic mission exists in your country.

Tip: Check the official EU visa policy list to confirm whether your nationality requires a Schengen visa before travelling to Malta.

Type C: Schengen Visa

The Schengen Type C visa allows you to stay in Malta — and other Schengen countries — for a maximum of 90 days within a 180-day period. This is suitable for short-term English language courses, summer programs, and intensive workshops. You cannot extend this visa to become a long-stay permit — if your program is longer, you need a Type D visa from the start.

Type D: National Long-Stay Visa

If your course is longer than 90 days — which includes all bachelor's, master's, and most full-year language programs — you need a National Type D visa. This is issued by the Maltese consulate in your home country and is your authorisation to enter Malta with the intention of obtaining a Student Residence Permit.

Student Residence Permit

The Student Residence Permit is the core document that legally authorises you to live and study in Malta for the duration of your program. It is applied for after you arrive in Malta, at Identità. It is issued for one year and is renewable annually, as long as you remain enrolled in your studies.

4. Malta Student Visa Requirements: Full Documents Checklist

The exact documents required depend on whether you are applying for a Schengen visa, a Type D visa, or the residence permit. The list below covers the core requirements shared across all three processes.

Mandatory Documents for All Students

  • Valid passport — with at least 3 months' validity beyond your intended stay and at least 2 blank pages
  • Acceptance letter — official letter from an approved Maltese educational institution confirming your enrolment
  • Proof of sufficient funds — bank statements showing at least €500–€800 per month of your stay, or a sponsor's financial guarantee letter
  • Health insurance — comprehensive medical insurance valid in Malta, minimum coverage of €30,000 (required for visa); for the residence permit, the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) may be accepted for EU nationals
  • Proof of accommodation — signed rental agreement, university hall confirmation, or a host family letter
  • Clean criminal record certificate — issued by the police authority in your home country, apostilled or legalised
  • Medical certificate — confirming you are free from contagious diseases
  • Biometric passport photos — recent, to Schengen or Maltese specifications (35×45mm, white background)
  • Completed application form — Schengen visa application form for Type C; Malta-specific form for Type D and residence permit
  • Application fee payment — proof of payment of the relevant fee

Additional Documents for the Student Residence Permit

  • Original enrollment certificate from your institution (updated for each renewal)
  • Evidence of previous academic qualifications (transcripts, diplomas)
  • Completed Identity Malta application form (available at Identità offices)
  • Recent utility bill or lease agreement confirming your Malta address
Important: All documents not in English or Maltese must be officially translated by a certified translator. Criminal records and birth certificates often require apostille legalisation. Allow 2–4 weeks for certified translations and apostille processing.

5. How to Apply for a Malta Student Visa: Step-by-Step

The Malta student visa process has two phases: the pre-departure phase (applying in your home country) and the post-arrival phase (obtaining the residence permit inside Malta). Here is the complete step-by-step breakdown.

Phase 1: Before You Leave Your Home Country

1

Choose and Apply to an Approved Institution

Your first step is selecting a course at an approved Maltese educational institution. Universities, colleges, and language schools in Malta must be licensed by the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA) or the Malta Tourism Authority (for language schools). Once accepted, you will receive an official acceptance letter — this is the cornerstone of your visa application.

2

Gather Your Documents

Collect all required documents listed in Section 4. Start early — criminal record certificates, apostilles, and certified translations typically take 2–6 weeks to obtain. Ensure your passport is valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned return date.

3

Book an Appointment at the Maltese Embassy or Consulate

Locate the nearest Maltese embassy or consulate. If Malta has no diplomatic mission in your country, look for an embassy that represents Malta for Schengen visa purposes (often Italy, France, or Germany). Book your appointment well in advance — popular consulates can be booked 3–6 weeks ahead.

4

Submit Your Application and Pay the Fee

Attend your appointment with all original documents and copies. Submit your application form, pay the visa fee (approximately €80 for Schengen), and provide biometric data (fingerprints and photo) if required. Keep all receipts and submission confirmation documents.

5

Wait for Visa Decision

Processing time is typically 15 calendar days for a Schengen visa and 4–8 weeks for a Type D long-stay visa. Do not book non-refundable flights or accommodation until your visa is confirmed. Track your application status through the consulate's tracking system where available.

6

Collect Your Visa and Travel to Malta

Once approved, collect your passport with the visa sticker. Check all details carefully — name spelling, validity dates, and number of entries must all be correct. Travel to Malta before your visa's start date and bring all original documents with you.

Phase 2: After You Arrive in Malta

7

Apply for Your Student Residence Permit at Identità

Within 3 months of your arrival, you must apply for a Student Residence Permit at Identità (formerly Identity Malta). Book an appointment online through the Identità portal. Bring all original documents plus the application form. Your residence permit card is typically ready within 4–8 weeks after submission.

8

Collect Your Residence Permit Card

Once notified, collect your biometric residence permit card from Identità. This card is your legal proof of the right to live and study in Malta. Keep it safe and carry it with you — it is required for banking, employment, and re-entry into Malta.

9

Renew Annually

The Student Residence Permit is valid for one year. You must renew it before it expires, submitting a fresh enrolment certificate from your institution and updated proof of funds. Begin the renewal process at least 6 weeks before expiry.

Timeline: Plan Your Application

If your course starts in September, here is a recommended timeline:

  • March–April: Apply to your chosen institution and obtain your acceptance letter
  • April–May: Gather all documents, apostilles, and translations
  • May: Submit visa application at the Maltese consulate
  • June–July: Receive visa decision
  • August: Book accommodation, flights, and travel to Malta
  • September: Arrive and start your studies
  • Within 3 months of arrival: Apply for Student Residence Permit at Identità
Do not leave it late: Apply for your visa at least 3 months before your course start date. Consulate appointment slots can be scarce, and processing can take longer than expected during peak summer periods.

8. The Malta Student Residence Permit Explained

The Student Residence Permit is the most important document you will hold as an international student in Malta. It is not a visa — it is a separate authorisation issued by the Maltese government that confirms your legal right to reside in Malta as a student.

Where to Apply

Identità — Malta's national identity and residence authority — handles all residence permit applications. Their main office is in Valletta, with additional service points across the island. Appointments must be booked online through their official portal.

What the Permit Allows

  • Legal residence in Malta for the duration of your studies
  • Freedom to travel within the Schengen Area (short stays)
  • Access to work rights (up to 20 hours per week during term)
  • Access to certain public services
  • Ability to open a bank account in Malta
  • Ability to rent accommodation officially

Renewing Your Residence Permit

The permit is issued for one academic year at a time. You must renew it before it expires — typically at the start of each new academic year. Bring a fresh enrolment certificate from your institution, updated bank statements, and current accommodation proof. Begin the renewal process at least 6 weeks before expiry.

If Your Permit Is Refused

Refusals are rare if your documents are complete and your institution is approved. Common reasons for refusal include insufficient funds, invalid health insurance, or an unrecognised institution. You have the right to appeal a refusal decision — contact Identità directly for the appeals procedure.

10. Working While Studying in Malta

One of the most asked questions from prospective international students is whether they can work in Malta while studying. The answer is yes — with conditions.

Work Rights for Non-EU Students

  • During term time: Up to 20 hours per week
  • During official holiday periods: Full-time (unlimited hours)
  • Requirement: You must hold a valid Student Residence Permit
  • Work permit: A separate work permit may be required from Jobsplus (Malta's employment authority) — your employer typically handles this

Popular Student Jobs in Malta

  • Hospitality and tourism (hotels, restaurants, bars)
  • Retail and customer service
  • English teaching assistant roles
  • iGaming support and customer service (English-speaking roles)
  • Administrative and data entry roles
  • Freelance and remote work

11. After Graduation: Post-Study Options in Malta

Completing your studies in Malta does not mean you must leave immediately. Malta offers several pathways for graduates who wish to remain and build their career.

Post-Study Work Permit

International graduates from Maltese institutions can apply for a post-study work permit, allowing them to remain in Malta and seek employment in their field. This is processed through Jobsplus and typically grants an initial stay of 12 months.

Malta's Job Market for Graduates

Malta has one of the lowest unemployment rates in the EU. Key growth sectors for international graduates include:

  • iGaming and online gaming — Malta is the global hub for regulated iGaming, with over 250 licensed operators based on the island
  • Financial services and fintech
  • Technology and software development
  • Tourism, hospitality, and aviation
  • Healthcare and pharmaceutical

Permanent Residency in Malta

After working legally in Malta for several years, graduates may qualify for long-term residence or permanent residence under EU free movement regulations. Malta offers several residence-by-investment and standard residence pathways for non-EU nationals who have established themselves in the country.

14. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa to study in Malta?
EU, EEA, and Swiss nationals do not need a visa. Non-EU nationals need a Schengen Type C visa for courses under 90 days, or a National Type D visa for programs longer than 90 days, followed by a Student Residence Permit once in Malta.
How much does the Malta student visa cost?
A Schengen visa costs approximately €80. A Type D long-stay visa costs €100–€150. The Student Residence Permit application at Identità costs approximately €27.50, plus the cost of the permit card. Total first-year visa costs typically range from €300 to €600 including insurance and documentation.
How long does the Malta student visa take to process?
A Schengen Type C visa takes 15 calendar days (up to 45 in complex cases). A Type D long-stay visa takes 4–8 weeks. The Student Residence Permit from Identità takes a further 4–8 weeks after submission in Malta.
Can I work in Malta on a student visa?
Yes. Non-EU students with a valid Student Residence Permit can work up to 20 hours per week during term time, and full-time during official holiday periods.
What bank balance do I need for a Malta student visa?
There is no fixed official minimum, but authorities typically expect proof of €500–€800 per month of intended stay — roughly €6,000–€9,600 for a full academic year. Funds can be your own savings or a sponsor's financial guarantee.
Can I extend my Malta student visa?
You cannot extend a Schengen Type C visa inside Malta. For longer stays, you must have applied for a Type D visa from the outset. The Student Residence Permit is renewed annually as long as you remain enrolled in your studies.
Is English required to study in Malta?
Most programs at Maltese institutions are taught in English. Some programs require proof of English proficiency (IELTS 5.5–6.5 or TOEFL equivalent). Malta is also an excellent destination for English language courses if you need to improve before starting a degree.
Can I stay in Malta after finishing my studies?
Yes. Graduates can apply for a post-study work permit, allowing them to remain in Malta and seek employment. Malta's strong iGaming, tech, and financial services sectors offer excellent graduate job opportunities.
