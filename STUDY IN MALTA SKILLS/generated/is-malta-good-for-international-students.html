The Verdict: Is Malta Good for International Students?

Malta is genuinely good for international students — but it is not the right fit for everyone. It excels in four specific areas: EU-recognised qualifications, English-medium instruction, affordable living costs, and personal safety. If those priorities align with your goals, Malta is a strong choice.

Where Malta is weaker: it is a small island with limited programme diversity compared to large European capitals, and the rental market has become competitive in recent years. Students who want the academic variety of London, the nightlife of Berlin, or the scale of Paris will find Malta modest by comparison.

But for students who want a quality European education in a safe, English-speaking environment without paying UK or Irish tuition fees, Malta consistently delivers. Outreach Recruitment has helped over 2,000 international students navigate the process, and the most common feedback is that Malta exceeded their expectations in terms of welcome, safety, and real-world opportunity.

Pros and Cons of Studying in Malta

Before diving into detail, here is a balanced, honest overview of the advantages and disadvantages of Malta as a study destination for international students.

✅ Pros

  • EU member state — Bologna-compliant, globally recognised degrees
  • English is an official language — no language barrier
  • Affordable tuition: €3,000–€12,000 per year
  • Safe — top 20 globally on the 2024 Global Peace Index
  • Mediterranean climate: 300+ sunny days per year
  • Right to work 20 hours/week (non-EU students)
  • Schengen Area access for travel within Europe
  • Welcoming, multicultural student community
  • Clear, well-documented visa process through Identità
  • EU residency pathway for long-term residents

⚠️ Cons

  • Small island — fewer programme options than major European capitals
  • Competitive rental market — accommodation can be hard to find
  • Public transport has improved but is still limited in rural areas
  • Summer temperatures above 35°C may feel intense for some
  • Fewer international university rankings (less brand recognition)
  • Limited scholarship funding compared to Germany or the Netherlands
  • Island logistics — travel to mainland Europe requires a flight

This balanced picture matters. Malta is not perfect — but for the right student profile, the advantages significantly outweigh the limitations. We will examine each of these points in depth below.

Education Quality and Recognition

Malta's higher education system is regulated by the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA) and aligned with the Bologna Process, making all Maltese degrees officially recognised across all 27 EU member states and in over 100 countries worldwide.

The University of Malta, founded in 1592, is the oldest university in the Mediterranean region. It offers bachelor's, master's, and doctoral programmes across law, science, engineering, medicine, arts, and business. Degrees follow a 3+1+3 structure (bachelor's + optional honours year + master's) and are fully compatible with European credit transfer standards (ECTS).

MCAST (Malta College of Arts, Science and Technology) is the main vocational and technical institution, offering higher national diplomas and bachelor's equivalents that are particularly strong in engineering, IT, and applied sciences.

Is the University of Malta Internationally Accredited?

Yes. The University of Malta holds membership in the Association of Commonwealth Universities (ACU), Coimbra Group, and the European University Association (EUA). Its qualifications appear on the UK's ENIC-NARIC recognised institutions list. Graduates have gone on to employment and postgraduate study at institutions across the EU, UK, USA, Canada, and Australia.

English-Medium Study Environment

English is one of Malta's two official languages and the primary language of instruction in virtually all bachelor's and master's programmes at Maltese universities. This is one of Malta's most powerful advantages for international students — it removes the language barrier that forces students in Germany, France, or Italy to spend years mastering a new language before beginning academic study.

Is English Enough to Study and Live in Malta?

Yes, English is entirely sufficient for both academic study and everyday life in Malta. Government services, healthcare, banking, supermarkets, public transport, and most workplaces operate in English alongside Maltese. Students from India, Nigeria, Libya, the Philippines, and dozens of other English-speaking countries find the transition remarkably smooth.

Academic English requirements vary by institution. The University of Malta typically requires IELTS 6.0–6.5 or equivalent. Private colleges and English language schools have lower thresholds and often offer foundation programmes to help students meet entry requirements.

English Language Courses in Malta

Malta is one of Europe's leading English Language Teaching (ELT) destinations, with over 40 accredited English language schools operating across the islands. Courses range from short intensive programmes (2–4 weeks) to year-long academic preparation courses. This makes Malta an excellent entry point for students who want to improve their English before progressing to degree-level study.

Cost of Studying in Malta 2026

Malta is one of the most affordable EU study destinations, with tuition fees and living costs substantially below the UK, Ireland, and the Netherlands. Here is a realistic breakdown for 2026.

Tuition Fees at Maltese Universities

Tuition fees depend on the institution, programme level, and your nationality. EU students often pay significantly reduced fees at the University of Malta (sometimes as low as €0 for EU nationals on government-sponsored programmes). Non-EU students typically pay full international fees ranging from €3,000 to €12,000 per academic year.

Visa and Residence Permit

EU and EEA nationals do not need a visa to study in Malta. Non-EU students studying for more than 90 days must obtain a Malta student visa (Type D long-stay visa) and a student residence permit through Identità (formerly Identity Malta). The process is well-documented and manageable with proper preparation.

What Applies to You?

For EU / EEA Students

As an EU or EEA citizen, you have the right to live and study in Malta without a visa. You may register your residence at the local council (Kunsill Lokali) and apply for a Maltese ID card if staying longer than 3 months. You have full work rights — no restriction on hours worked per week. Erasmus+ grants are available if your home university participates in the programme.

For Non-EU Students

Non-EU students must apply for a Malta student visa and student residence permit. Core requirements include: a letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution, proof of financial means (approximately €7,000 per year), comprehensive health insurance, a valid passport, a clear criminal record certificate, and proof of accommodation. Applications are submitted to Identità (Malta) and typically processed within 4–8 weeks. Students can work up to 20 hours per week during term and full-time during official academic holidays.

Apply for your student place first — then submit your visa application at least 3 months before your course start date.

Safety and Quality of Life

Malta consistently ranks among the safest countries in the world. The 2024 Global Peace Index placed Malta at position 18 globally — ahead of Germany, France, and the United States. For international students arriving alone in a new country, this matters enormously.

Violent crime in Malta is extremely rare. The local population is known for being friendly and helpful to visitors and students. The islands are small enough that you can walk or take a bus almost anywhere, and the warm Mediterranean climate makes outdoor life enjoyable for most of the year.

Healthcare for International Students in Malta

Malta has a public healthcare system (Mater Dei Hospital) that provides emergency care to all residents. EU students with a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) can access public healthcare under the same conditions as Maltese citizens. Non-EU students are required to hold private health insurance as part of their visa conditions — policies start from around €30/month and are widely available from Maltese insurers.

Climate and Lifestyle

Malta has one of the sunniest climates in Europe, with over 300 days of sunshine per year and warm sea temperatures from May through November. Summer temperatures can exceed 35°C, which some students from cooler climates find challenging. Winters are mild, rarely dropping below 10°C, making year-round outdoor study and social life possible.

Student Accommodation in Malta

Finding affordable student accommodation is one of the main challenges international students report in Malta. The rental market has become competitive, especially in areas close to the University of Malta (Msida) and popular social hubs (Sliema, St Julian's, Valletta). Plan ahead and begin your search at least 2–3 months before your course starts.

Working While Studying in Malta

Non-EU students on a student residence permit in Malta are entitled to work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official academic holidays. This part-time work right is a significant financial benefit that helps students cover living costs without relying entirely on savings or family support.

Common Jobs for International Students in Malta

  • Hospitality & tourism — hotels, restaurants, bars (especially in Sliema and St Julian's)
  • Retail — shops and supermarkets in tourist areas and town centres
  • English language teaching support — classroom assistants at ELT schools
  • iGaming and fintech — Malta hosts dozens of licensed iGaming companies; entry-level support roles often available to multilingual students
  • Tutoring — private tuition in English, maths, or other academic subjects
  • Call centres / customer support — English-speaking roles at international companies based in Malta

Minimum wage in Malta in 2026 is approximately €950/month for full-time work. Part-time rates vary by sector but typically range from €7.50 to €10.00 per hour. Working 20 hours per week at the average student job rate can generate €600–€700/month — enough to cover rent in a shared flat.

Career and Post-Study Opportunities

Graduating in Malta opens doors across the EU and globally. Because degrees are Bologna-compliant and EU-recognised, graduates can apply for professional positions and postgraduate programmes across all 27 EU member states without needing credential evaluations.

Can I Stay in Malta After Graduating?

Non-EU graduates of Maltese institutions can apply to stay in Malta for work after graduation. Malta has introduced post-study work provisions that allow graduates to remain in the country while seeking employment in their field. Many students also progress to full employment at companies that offered them part-time work during their studies.

Malta's economy is dominated by financial services, iGaming, tourism, maritime, and increasingly by technology and healthcare. The iGaming sector alone employs thousands of English-speaking professionals, many of whom are international graduates from Maltese institutions. The island's small size means professional networks form quickly, and career transitions from study to work are often smoother than in larger European cities.

EU citizenship pathway: After 5 years of legal continuous residence in Malta, non-EU nationals may be eligible to apply for Maltese citizenship through naturalisation, subject to meeting language, income, and integration requirements. This is a significant long-term benefit for students who choose to build their careers in Malta.

Student Life in Malta

Student life in Malta is relaxed, social, and Mediterranean. The international student community is diverse — students from over 100 countries study in Malta each year, creating a genuinely multicultural environment. You are likely to share classes, cafés, and bus stops with fellow students from India, Nigeria, Libya, Tunisia, Italy, the UK, and beyond.

Things to Do as a Student in Malta

  • Beach & sea: Malta has over 60 beaches and crystal-clear water within easy reach — Golden Bay, Mellieha Bay, and the Blue Lagoon on Comino are favourites
  • History & culture: Valletta is a UNESCO World Heritage Site; Mdina, the ancient walled city, and the megalithic temples of Ggantija predate Stonehenge
  • Nightlife: Paceville in St Julian's is Malta's main entertainment district, with clubs, bars, and restaurants open late
  • Travel: Malta's Schengen membership means easy weekend travel to Sicily (ferry, 90 min), Rome (flight, 1h 15m), or beyond
  • Sports: water polo, diving, and football are popular; the University of Malta has student sports clubs across many disciplines

The pace of life in Malta is slower than major European capitals — which many students see as a benefit. The island is small enough that the commute from almost anywhere to anywhere takes under 45 minutes by bus. There is no rush-hour subway crush or urban isolation. Students consistently describe Malta as a place where it is easy to build community quickly.

Who Is Malta Best For?

Based on the experiences of 2,000+ students guided by Outreach Recruitment, Malta is the strongest fit for these student profiles:

  • Students who want an EU-recognised degree without paying UK or Irish tuition prices — Malta delivers Bologna-compliant qualifications at a fraction of the cost
  • Non-EU students from English-speaking countries — Nigeria, Ghana, India, the Philippines, Libya, Egypt, and South Africa — who want a smooth academic transition without a language hurdle
  • Students who value personal safety — Malta is one of the safest places in the world
  • Students interested in building a career in Europe — EU degree + right to stay post-graduation = strong long-term pathway
  • Students who want to study English as a second language in a native-speaking environment before progressing to degree-level study
  • Older or mature students who prefer a quieter, community-oriented environment over a large, impersonal campus city

Malta is a weaker fit for students who prioritise elite global university rankings (Times Higher Education top 100), a large metropolis with major cultural institutions, or a wide choice of highly specialised postgraduate research programmes.

How to Apply to Study in Malta

Applying to study in Malta is a clear, step-by-step process. Most students complete the full cycle — from enquiry to visa approval — within 3 to 4 months.

  1. Choose your programme and institution. Use the full programme table to compare options by level, subject, and institution. Confirm the institution holds an MFHEA licence.
  2. Submit your application. Complete the institution's application form and provide academic transcripts, a personal statement, and a copy of your passport.
  3. Receive your Letter of Acceptance (LOA). The institution issues an official acceptance letter — this is the key document for your visa application.
  4. Apply for your student visa (non-EU only). Submit your visa application to the Maltese embassy or consulate in your country, or directly through Identità (Malta). Include your LOA, financial proof, health insurance, passport, and criminal record certificate.
  5. Arrange accommodation. Secure a place to stay before arriving. Start your search at least 2–3 months before your course begins.
  6. Arrive and register. On arrival, complete your student residence permit application (if non-EU) and register with your institution. Collect your student ID and Tallinja transport card.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Malta good for international students?

Yes. Malta is an EU member state with English as an official language, offering Bologna-recognised degrees, affordable tuition fees, and a straightforward student visa process. Students benefit from Mediterranean living, a safe environment, part-time work rights (up to 20 hours/week), and real post-study career opportunities across the EU.

What are the pros and cons of studying in Malta?

Pros include EU-recognised degrees, English instruction, affordable tuition (€3,000–€12,000/year), safety (top 20 globally), and part-time work rights. Cons include the island's small size (fewer programme options), a competitive rental market, limited public transport in some areas, and fewer global university rankings compared to major European capitals.

Is Malta safe for international students?

Malta is one of the safest countries in the world. The 2024 Global Peace Index ranked Malta 18th globally — ahead of Germany, France, and the USA. Violent crime is extremely rare. The local community is known for being welcoming and helpful to international students.

How much does it cost to study in Malta per year?

Tuition fees range from €3,000 to €12,000 per year depending on the programme and institution. Monthly living costs average €700–€1,200, including rent, food, transport, and health insurance. A realistic total annual budget for a non-EU student is €10,000–€20,000 — significantly below comparable EU destinations in Western Europe.

Do I need a visa to study in Malta?

EU and EEA students do not need a visa. Non-EU students studying for more than 90 days must obtain a Malta student visa (Type D) and a student residence permit through Identità (Identity Malta). Core requirements include a letter of acceptance, financial proof, health insurance, a valid passport, and a clean criminal record.

Can international students work in Malta?

Non-EU students on a student residence permit can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official academic holidays. EU students have full work rights with no hour restriction. Part-time roles are common in hospitality, retail, iGaming support, and English language teaching.

Is the University of Malta recognised internationally?

Yes. The University of Malta holds membership in the Association of Commonwealth Universities (ACU), the Coimbra Group, and the European University Association (EUA). Its degrees are Bologna-compliant and recognised across all EU member states, the UK (ENIC-NARIC listed), and in many countries internationally.

Are there scholarships for international students in Malta?

Yes. Options include Erasmus+ grants (for EU students at participating institutions), Malta Government Scholarship Scheme (MGSS), University of Malta merit bursaries, and institution-specific scholarships. Scholarship funding in Malta is more limited than in Germany or the Netherlands, so proactive applications are important.
