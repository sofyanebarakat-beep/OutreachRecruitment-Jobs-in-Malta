Guide to Study in Malta 2026: Everything International Students Need to Know

Quick Answer

Malta is a full EU member state where international students can pursue bachelor's, master's, and English language programs at the University of Malta, MCAST, and private colleges. Non-EU students need a student visa and residence permit. Tuition fees range from €3,000 to €12,000 per year; living costs average €800–€1,100 per month. English is an official language, making Malta one of the most accessible study destinations in Europe for 2026.

Key facts: EU membership · English official language · Mediterranean climate · Affordable tuition · Post-study work rights · Safe & welcoming culture

Malta's Higher Education System Explained

Malta's education system is built on the British model and is fully aligned with the Bologna Process, meaning degrees are recognized across all EU member states and in many countries worldwide. The Maltese Qualifications Framework (MQF) maps directly to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF), which gives Maltese credentials genuine international weight.

Higher education in Malta is overseen by the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA), the official regulatory body responsible for licensing and quality assurance of all further and higher education institutions. Before enrolling at any institution, verify that it carries a valid MFHEA license — this protects your qualification's recognition and your visa eligibility.

Types of Qualifications

  • Certificate / Diploma — MQF levels 1–5, typically 1–2 years
  • Bachelor's Degree — MQF level 6, typically 3–4 years
  • Master's Degree — MQF level 7, typically 1–2 years
  • Doctoral Degree (PhD) — MQF level 8, typically 3–4 years
  • English Language Courses — from 2 weeks to 12 months, all levels

All mainstream programs at public universities are taught in English, making Malta uniquely accessible for international students who do not speak Maltese.

Top Universities and Colleges in Malta in 2026

University of Malta (UM)

Founded in 1592, the University of Malta is the oldest and most prestigious institution on the island. It offers over 200 programs across 14 faculties, covering arts, sciences, engineering, law, medicine, economics, ICT, and more. The university is publicly funded and ranked among Southern Europe's established institutions. Its main campus is in Msida, with a second campus in Gozo.

  • Location: Msida & Gozo
  • Programs: Bachelor's, Master's, PhD
  • Language: English
  • Website: um.edu.mt

MCAST — Malta College of Arts, Science and Technology

MCAST is Malta's main vocational and technical college, offering a wide range of applied diplomas, higher national diplomas, and bachelor's-level qualifications. It has six institutes covering engineering, ICT, business, creative arts, health, and maritime studies. MCAST is popular with students who prefer a hands-on, skills-focused approach.

Malta Student Visa Requirements 2026

Visa requirements differ based on your nationality:

EU / EEA / Swiss Citizens

If you hold a passport from an EU, EEA, or Swiss country, you do not need a student visa to study in Malta. You can enroll in any accredited institution and reside in Malta freely under freedom of movement rules. You should, however, register your residence at the local Identity Malta office (now Identità) within three months of arrival.

Non-EU Citizens — Malta Student Visa (Type D)

Non-EU international students must apply for a Malta Long Stay Visa (Type D) before arriving. This is combined with the student residence permit application and is processed through the Maltese embassy or consulate in your home country, or in some cases through Identità in Malta.

Documents Required for the Malta Student Visa

  1. Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity beyond your intended stay)
  2. Completed visa/permit application form
  3. Official letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution
  4. Proof of financial means — typically a minimum of €7,000 per year (bank statements, sponsor letter, or scholarship award letter)
  5. Proof of accommodation in Malta (rental agreement or student residence confirmation)
  6. Comprehensive health insurance covering the full duration of your studies
  7. Clean criminal record certificate (apostilled)
  8. Academic transcripts and diplomas (apostilled or notarized)
  9. Passport-size photographs
  10. Visa/permit fee payment
2026 update: Malta has introduced a digitized application portal for student permits. Many document submissions can now be made online via the Identità e-services portal. Check with your institution's admissions team for the latest process at the time of application.

Student Residence Permit in Malta

For programs lasting more than 90 days, non-EU students must hold a student residence permit issued by Identità (formerly Identity Malta). This permit is tied to your enrollment at a licensed institution and must be renewed annually (or aligned with your academic year).

How to Apply for the Student Residence Permit

  1. Receive your official acceptance letter from a licensed Maltese institution
  2. Gather the required documents (as listed under visa requirements above)
  3. Submit your application via the Identità portal or at an Identità office
  4. Attend a biometric appointment (fingerprints and photo)
  5. Receive your residence permit card, typically valid for 12 months
  6. Renew annually before expiry, as long as you remain enrolled

Permit Conditions

  • You must remain enrolled at the institution named on your permit
  • You must notify Identità if you change institution
  • Failure to renew on time can result in unlawful stay and complications for future applications
  • The permit is not a work permit by default — see working rights below

Scholarships for International Students in Malta 2026

Funding your studies in Malta is more achievable than many students realize. Several national, EU-level, and institutional scholarship programs exist for international students:

1. Erasmus+ (EU Students)

The Erasmus+ program supports EU students studying abroad within Europe. If you are enrolled in a university in another EU country and wish to spend a semester or year at the University of Malta, an Erasmus+ grant can cover travel and partially offset living costs. Grants typically range from €200–€800 per month depending on your home country.

2. Malta Government Scholarship Scheme (MGSS)

The Malta Government Scholarship Scheme, administered by Scholarships Malta, provides funding for both Maltese nationals and, in some programs, international students from specific countries with bilateral agreements. Categories include full scholarships, partial grants, and research fellowships at the PhD level.

3. University of Malta Bursaries

The University of Malta offers merit-based and needs-based bursaries through its Student Services department. International students enrolled in accredited programs may apply. Awards vary but can reduce tuition by 10–30%.

4. Commonwealth Scholarships

Malta, as a Commonwealth member, participates in several Commonwealth scholarship programs. These are primarily available to students from Commonwealth developing nations and cover full tuition and living expenses at postgraduate level.

5. Institutional & Private Scholarships

Many private colleges and MCAST offer discounts or partial scholarships for early applicants, high-achieving students, or students from specific regions. Always ask your admissions contact about available incentives.

Student Accommodation in Malta

Finding accommodation in Malta as an international student is manageable, but the market can move fast — especially in September when the academic year begins. Start your search at least two months in advance.

Types of Accommodation

University Residences

The University of Malta operates the University Residence at Lija, offering en-suite rooms with communal facilities. These fill quickly. Apply directly through the University's Student Services portal as early as possible.

Private Shared Apartments

The most common option for international students is renting a room in a shared apartment. Popular areas near the University include Msida, Gzira, Sliema, St. Julian's, and Birkirkara. Costs range from €350–€600 per month for a furnished room including bills.

Homestays

Particularly popular among English language students, homestays offer a room and meals with a Maltese family. This immersive option costs €500–€850 per month and provides both accommodation and a cultural experience.

Student Villages (Private)

Several private student villages have opened in Malta in recent years, offering purpose-built student rooms with Wi-Fi, gym, study areas, and social spaces. These tend to cost €550–€900 per month but include more amenities.

Working While Studying in Malta in 2026

Malta's rules on working while studying depend on your nationality:

EU / EEA Students

EU and EEA students have the right to work without restriction in Malta. You can work full-time, part-time, or on a freelance basis alongside your studies, with no hours cap.

Non-EU Students

Non-EU students holding a valid student residence permit are permitted to work up to 20 hours per week during term time. During official university holidays and summer, full-time work is generally permitted, subject to your employer obtaining the appropriate permit documentation from Jobsplus (Malta's employment authority).

Popular Student Jobs in Malta

  • Hospitality and tourism (hotels, restaurants, bars)
  • Customer service and call centers
  • Retail and shop assistant roles
  • English language teaching assistance (for native speakers)
  • ICT and tech support roles (for IT students)
  • Event and promotions work

Minimum wage in Malta in 2026 is €812.08/month (€4.68/hour for a 40-hour week), providing a basic income floor for student workers.

Post-Study Work Opportunities in Malta 2026

One of Malta's most compelling advantages for international students is what comes after graduation. Malta offers genuine post-study work pathways, and its EU membership means a Maltese qualification carries weight across the entire European labor market.

EU Graduates

EU graduates who studied in Malta can remain and work without any restriction. Malta's growing economy — particularly in iGaming, fintech, ICT, hospitality, financial services, and healthcare — means opportunities are available for qualified graduates.

Non-EU Graduates

Non-EU graduates who completed a degree at a Maltese institution can apply to convert their student permit into a work permit through Jobsplus. This is not automatic — it requires a job offer from a Maltese employer — but Malta's labor market is active and many employers actively recruit from the international graduate pool.

Student Life in Malta

Life as a student in Malta is genuinely enjoyable. The island is safe, warm, English-speaking, and richly historic — the capital Valletta is a UNESCO World Heritage city and was the European Capital of Culture in 2018.

Climate

Malta enjoys over 300 days of sunshine per year. Summers are hot (30–35°C), winters mild (12–18°C). Studying here means you can realistically swim in the sea from May to November.

Safety

Malta consistently ranks as one of the safest countries in the EU. Violent crime rates are extremely low. Solo students and young women report feeling safe traveling and living here.

Getting Around

Malta has a comprehensive bus network operated by Malta Public Transport. A student monthly pass costs approximately €26. The island is small — you can reach the beach, the university, or the historic city center within 30–45 minutes from most residential areas.

Culture and Nightlife

St. Julian's (specifically Paceville) is Malta's entertainment hub, with bars, clubs, and restaurants popular with students and young professionals. Valletta and Sliema offer a more refined cultural and dining experience. Gozo, Malta's sister island, is a popular weekend escape.

Sports and Outdoor Life

Diving, snorkeling, hiking, football, water polo, and cycling are all popular among students. The University of Malta has sports facilities on campus, and dozens of clubs and societies cater to student interests.

How to Apply to Study in Malta: Step-by-Step Guide

  1. Choose your institution and program — Research MFHEA-licensed institutions and select a program that matches your academic background and career goals. Use the programs table to browse all available options.
  2. Check entry requirements — Confirm the academic qualifications, English proficiency scores (IELTS 5.5–6.5 is typically required), and any subject-specific prerequisites.
  3. Submit your application — Apply directly through the institution's admissions portal or through an authorized agent such as Outreach Recruitment.
  4. Receive your acceptance letter — This is the official letter of acceptance (LOA) you will need for your visa application.

  6. Arrange accommodation — Book your accommodation before traveling. Start looking at least 8 weeks before your course start date.
  7. Arrive and register — Complete enrollment at your institution and register your address with Identità within the required timeframe.
  8. Collect your residence permit card — Attend your biometrics appointment and collect your permit card.
  9. Begin your studies — Attend orientation, access student support services, and start your academic journey in Malta.

Common Mistakes International Students Make When Applying to Study in Malta

  1. Enrolling in an unlicensed institution — Always verify MFHEA licensing. An unlicensed institution cannot support a valid student permit application.
  2. Leaving the visa application too late — Processing takes 4–8 weeks minimum. Apply well before your course start date.
  3. Underestimating costs — Budget for the first month upfront: deposit, first month's rent, insurance, and living expenses before your first paycheck.
  4. Not reading permit conditions — Your permit ties you to the institution named. Changing institution without notifying Identità can invalidate your permit.
  5. Missing permit renewal deadlines — Set a reminder 60 days before expiry. Late renewal creates unlawful stay status even if your course is still running.
  6. Assuming all English courses qualify — For permit purposes, your course must meet minimum duration requirements (typically 15 hours/week for study visa eligibility).
  7. Not using a qualified advisor — Navigating the admissions and permit process alone is possible but time-consuming. Using an authorized advisor like Outreach Recruitment reduces error and delays.

Frequently Asked Questions: Study in Malta 2026

Can international students study in Malta?

Yes. Malta welcomes international students from all countries. EU students enjoy freedom of movement. Non-EU students need a student visa and residence permit issued by Identità. Programs are widely taught in English, and Malta's institutions are MFHEA-licensed and internationally recognized.

What English level do I need to study in Malta?

Most undergraduate programs at the University of Malta require a minimum IELTS score of 5.5–6.0 (or equivalent). Master's programs typically require 6.0–6.5. Some private colleges accept alternative evidence of English proficiency, such as a previous degree taught in English.

Is it expensive to live in Malta as a student?

Relative to Western Europe, Malta is affordable. Budget approximately €750–€1,100 per month for accommodation, food, transport, and basic social activities. Sliema and St. Julian's are more expensive; areas like Birkirkara and Ħamrun are more budget-friendly.

Can I work while studying in Malta?

EU students can work without restriction. Non-EU students on a student permit can work up to 20 hours per week during term time. During official holidays, full-time work is generally permitted. Your employer must notify Jobsplus.

How long does the Malta student residence permit take?

Processing takes 3–8 weeks depending on your application route. Applications submitted through the Identità portal are generally faster than embassy applications abroad. Apply at least 8 weeks before your course start date.

Are Maltese degrees recognized internationally?

Yes. Malta follows the Bologna Process, and degrees are recognized across all EU countries. The University of Malta is a member of the Coimbra Group and the European University Association. Non-EU recognition depends on your destination country's bilateral agreements, but Maltese qualifications are widely accepted.

What scholarships are available in Malta for international students?

Key scholarships include Erasmus+ (for EU students), the Malta Government Scholarship Scheme, University of Malta bursaries, Commonwealth Scholarships (for eligible nationals), and institutional scholarships from private colleges. Deadlines typically fall between November and March for the following academic year.

Is Malta safe for international students?

Malta is one of the safest countries in the EU. It consistently ranks in the top 10 globally for personal safety. The island has a very low violent crime rate, and students — including solo female students — report feeling secure in both urban and residential areas.
