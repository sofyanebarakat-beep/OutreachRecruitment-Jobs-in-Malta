Why Malta in 2026?

Malta is a full EU member state where all accredited programmes are taught in English — one of only two EU countries with English as an official language. It combines EU-recognised degrees, affordable tuition, Mediterranean quality of life, and a growing job market in iGaming, fintech, and ICT.

  • Regulator: MFHEA — verify licensing at mfhea.mt before enrolling
  • 300+ sunny days/year — one of Europe’s sunniest countries
  • Safe — consistently top 5 in EU safety rankings

1. Malta’s higher education system explained

Malta’s higher education is built on the British academic model and fully aligned with the Bologna Process. The Maltese Qualifications Framework (MQF) governs qualifications from level 1 to 8 and maps directly to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF). Every degree from a licensed Maltese institution carries verifiable EU recognition.

The regulatory body is the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA). A valid MFHEA licence is mandatory for your student residence permit — verify at mfhea.mt.

  • Certificate / Diploma — MQF 1–5, 1–2 years
  • Bachelor’s Degree — MQF 6, 3–4 years
  • Master’s Degree — MQF 7, 1–2 years
  • Doctoral Degree (PhD) — MQF 8, 3–4 years
  • English Language Courses — 2 weeks to 12 months, all CEFR levels
2. Top universities and colleges in Malta

Malta has one public university founded in 1592, one vocational college (MCAST), and a range of MFHEA-licensed private institutions — all teaching in English.

University of Malta (UM): Founded 1592. 200+ programmes across 14 faculties. Member of the EUA and Coimbra Group.

MCAST: Six institutes: Engineering, ICT, Business, Creative Arts, Health, and Maritime Studies.

  • GGFL — Finance, law, business
  • St. Martin’s Institute — ICT and business
  • IDEA Academy — Design and creative arts
  • AeSMI — Aviation and maritime certifications

3. Study programmes available in Malta

Malta offers English-taught programmes across all major disciplines. The most in-demand fields in 2026 are business, ICT, law, finance, health sciences, and maritime studies.

  • Business & Management — 3-year Bachelor’s; UM, MCAST, private colleges
  • Information Technology & AI — 3-year Bachelor’s; strong employer demand
  • Law & Legal Studies — 4-year Bachelor’s; EU law focus at UM and GGFL
  • Engineering & Technology — 4-year Bachelor’s; UM and MCAST
  • Health Sciences & Medicine — 5–6 year MD; UM only; competitive entry
  • Finance & Economics — 3-year Bachelor’s; Malta’s financial sector is a key employer
  • English Language Courses — 2 weeks to 12 months; 30+ EAQUALS/FELTOM-accredited schools
  • MBA & Master’s — 1–2 years; UM and private providers; part-time options available
4. Malta student visa requirements 2026

EU and EEA citizens need no visa. Non-EU students must apply for a Malta Long Stay Visa (Type D) combined with a student residence permit.

  1. Valid passport — minimum 6 months validity beyond your study period
  2. Completed application via the Identità e-services portal
  3. Official letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution
  4. Proof of financial means — minimum €7,000 per year
  5. Proof of accommodation — rental agreement or student residence confirmation
  6. Comprehensive health insurance for the full study period
  7. Clean criminal record certificate — apostilled, all countries of residence in past 5 years
  8. Academic transcripts and diplomas — apostilled or notarised; translated if not in English
  9. Passport-size photographs (biometric)
  10. Visa and permit fee payment

Processing times: Maltese embassy abroad: 4–8 weeks. Identità in Malta: 3–6 weeks. Apply at least 8 weeks before your course start date.

2026 note: Identità has expanded its e-services portal — biometric appointments can now be booked online.

5. Student residence permit in Malta

Non-EU students enrolled in programmes over 90 days must hold a student residence permit from Identità. The permit is tied to your enrolled institution and renewed annually.

  • Submit application via the Identità portal or at a service centre
  • Attend biometric appointment — fingerprints and photograph
  • Permit card valid 12 months; renew before expiry
  • Notify Identità immediately if you change institution
  • Late renewal = unlawful stay status — renew at least 30 days before expiry

6. Cost of studying in Malta in 2026

Malta’s living costs average €750–€1,100/month — significantly below London, Amsterdam, or Dublin. Tuition fees are also among the lowest in the EU for English-taught programmes.

  • University of Malta undergraduate: €3,000–€7,500/year
  • University of Malta postgraduate: €5,000–€12,000/year
  • MCAST diploma/HND: €2,500–€5,000/year
  • Private college undergraduate: €4,000–€10,000/year
  • English language school: €150–€350/week
  • Accommodation (shared): €350–€600/month
  • Food and groceries: €150–€250/month
  • Student transport pass: €26/month
  • Health insurance: €30–€80/month

7. Scholarships for international students in Malta 2026

Several national, EU-level, and institutional scholarship programmes exist specifically for international students in Malta, ranging from full tuition coverage to partial grants.

  • Erasmus+ — EU students on exchange; €200–€800/month
  • Malta Government Scholarship Scheme (MGSS) — full and partial grants; see scholarshipsmalta.gov.mt
  • University of Malta bursaries — 10–30% tuition reduction; merit and needs-based
  • Commonwealth Scholarships — full tuition and stipend; deadline November–December
8. Student accommodation in Malta

Start your accommodation search at least 8–10 weeks before your course. The market moves quickly, especially for September intake.

  • University Residence (Lija) — En-suite rooms; managed by UM; apply early
  • Shared apartment — Most common; Msida, Gzira, Sliema, St. Julian’s; €350–€600/month
  • Homestay — Room and meals; €500–€850/month; ideal for EFL students
  • Private student villages — Wi-Fi, gym, study spaces; €550–€900/month
9. Working while studying in Malta in 2026

EU students have no work restrictions. Non-EU students on a valid student residence permit may work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official academic holidays.

  • EU/EEA students — unlimited right to work
  • Non-EU students — up to 20 hrs/week during term; full-time in official holidays
  • Minimum wage 2026 — €812.08/month (40-hour week)
  • Popular roles — hospitality, call centres, retail, events, ICT support
10. Post-study work opportunities in Malta

Malta’s economy is one of the EU’s fastest-growing, driven by iGaming, fintech, financial services, and ICT. EU graduates can remain and work freely. Non-EU graduates can convert their permit via a job offer through Jobsplus.

  • iGaming and online betting — €28,000–€42,000/year
  • Financial services and fintech — €26,000–€40,000/year
  • ICT and software — €28,000–€50,000/year
  • Tourism and hospitality — €22,000–€32,000/year
11. English language courses in Malta

Malta is a world-leading EFL destination with 30+ EAQUALS/FELTOM-accredited schools. With English as an official language used daily, students experience genuine immersion outside the classroom.

  • General English (A1–C2): €150–€350/week
  • IELTS/Cambridge exam preparation: €600–€900 for 4 weeks
  • Business English, Academic English, intensive summer programmes
12. Student life in Malta

Living in Malta means Mediterranean weather, a compact island, a safe environment, and a rich cultural life — all while studying towards an EU-recognised degree.

  • Climate — 300+ sunny days/year; swimming season May–November
  • Safety — consistently top 5 safest EU countries
  • Transport — student monthly bus pass €26; island reachable in 30–45 minutes
  • Culture — Valletta is a UNESCO World Heritage City; Gozo (25 min ferry) is a weekend escape
  • Student community — University of Malta has 200+ clubs and societies

13. How to apply to study in Malta — step-by-step guide

Applying to study in Malta involves nine steps. Start at least three months before your intended course date to allow sufficient time for visa processing and accommodation.

  1. Choose your institution and programme — Research MFHEA-licensed institutions. View all available programmes on our 2026 listing.
  2. Check entry requirements — IELTS 5.5–6.0 for undergraduate; 6.0–6.5 for postgraduate.
  3. Submit your application — Apply through the institution’s admissions portal or via an authorised representative such as Outreach Recruitment.
  4. Receive your letter of acceptance (LOA) — Confirms programme, start date, and tuition. Cornerstone document for your visa.
  5. Apply for your visa and residence permit — Apply at least 8 weeks before your course date.
  6. Arrange accommodation — Book before arriving. Start searching 10 weeks out for September intake.
  7. Arrive and complete enrolment — Complete institutional enrolment and register your address with Identità.
  8. Attend biometric appointment — Book via the Identità e-services portal.
  9. Collect your residence permit card — Collect from Identità and begin your studies.
14. Common mistakes international students make
  1. Enrolling at an unlicensed institution — No MFHEA licence = no permit. Verify at mfhea.mt first.
  2. Applying for the visa too late — Processing takes 4–8 weeks minimum.
  3. Underestimating upfront costs — Budget for deposit, first month’s rent, insurance, and 4 weeks of living expenses before any income arrives.
  4. Working above permit hours — Exceeding 20 hrs/week as a non-EU student can invalidate your permit.
  5. Missing renewal deadlines — Renew at least 30 days before expiry.
  6. Assuming all English courses qualify — Courses under 15 hrs/week may not meet permit eligibility.
  7. Not using a qualified advisor — An authorised advisor reduces errors and delays significantly.
15. Malta vs other study destinations — honest comparison
  • Malta vs UK — Malta is significantly cheaper. UK degrees carry stronger brand recognition outside the EU.
  • Malta vs Germany — Germany has near-free tuition but most programmes require German. Malta teaches entirely in English.
  • Malta vs Netherlands — Similar English-language offering but Dutch living costs are 40–60% higher.
  • Malta vs Cyprus — Both are small EU English-speaking islands. Malta has a more established EFL industry and more diverse economy.
Frequently Asked Questions
Can international students study in Malta?

Yes. Malta welcomes international students from EU and non-EU countries. Non-EU students need a Type D student visa and a residence permit from Identità. All accredited programmes are taught in English and degrees are recognised across all 27 EU member states.

How much does it cost to study in Malta in 2026?

University of Malta tuition ranges from €3,000 to €7,500/year for undergraduates and €5,000 to €12,000/year for postgraduates. Private colleges charge €4,000 to €10,000/year. Monthly living costs average €750 to €1,100, covering accommodation, food, transport, and insurance.

What documents are needed for the Malta student visa?

You need: a valid passport, letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution, proof of financial means (€7,000+/year), proof of accommodation, comprehensive health insurance, an apostilled criminal record certificate, and academic transcripts. Processing takes 4–8 weeks.

Can I work while studying in Malta?

EU students have no work restrictions. Non-EU students on a valid student residence permit may work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official academic holidays. Employers must notify Jobsplus. Minimum wage in 2026 is €812.08/month.

Are Maltese degrees recognised internationally?

Yes. Malta follows the Bologna Process and the Maltese Qualifications Framework maps directly to the European Qualifications Framework. University of Malta degrees are recognised across all 27 EU member states and widely accepted by employers globally.

What scholarships are available in Malta?

Key scholarships: Erasmus+ (€200–€800/month for EU students), the Malta Government Scholarship Scheme, University of Malta bursaries (10–30% tuition reduction), and Commonwealth Scholarships. Deadlines fall between November and March annually.

Is Malta in the Schengen Area?

Yes. Malta joined the Schengen Area in 2007. A student residence permit allows travel within the Schengen zone for short stays — up to 90 days in any 180-day period in other Schengen countries — while you are enrolled in Malta.

Key takeaways
  • Malta is a full EU member state — degrees recognised across all 27 EU countries
  • English is an official language — no language barrier
  • Non-EU students need a Type D visa and residence permit from Identità
  • Tuition: €3,000–€12,000/year; living costs: €750–€1,100/month
  • Non-EU students can work up to 20 hrs/week during term time
  • Malta is in the Schengen Area — your permit enables free Schengen travel
  • Always verify MFHEA licensing before enrolling
  • Apply at least 8 weeks before your course start date
Conclusion

Malta in 2026 offers a fully EU-recognised degree, English as an official language, Mediterranean quality of life, affordable costs, and an active post-graduation job market. Few places in Europe offer this combination simultaneously.
