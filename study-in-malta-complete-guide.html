Guide to Study in Malta 2026: Complete Guide for International Students

June 2026 — By Sofyane Barakat · Recruitment & International Education Specialist, Malta
Quick Answer — What is studying in Malta like in 2026?

Malta is a full EU member state where international students can pursue English-taught bachelor's, master's, and language programmes at the University of Malta, MCAST, and MFHEA-licensed private colleges. Non-EU students need a student visa and residence permit. Tuition fees range from €3,000 to €12,000 per year; living costs average €750–€1,100/month. English is an official language — no language barrier. Degrees are recognised across all 27 EU member states.

Why Malta is Europe's most underrated study destination in 2026

Malta has quietly become one of Europe's most compelling study destinations — and in 2026, the case for choosing this Mediterranean island nation is stronger than ever. It offers something genuinely rare: full European Union membership, English as an official language (one of only two EU countries), affordable tuition, and an internationally recognised higher education system built on the Bologna Process.

For a decade, Malta was largely overlooked in favour of the UK, Germany, or the Netherlands. Today, with rising tuition fees across Western Europe, stricter post-Brexit visa rules for non-EU students in the UK, and a growing awareness of Malta's unique advantages, thousands of international students from Africa, Asia, the Middle East, and the Americas are choosing Malta every year.

This complete guide covers everything you need to know: universities, visa requirements, costs, scholarships, accommodation, working rights, post-study opportunities, and a step-by-step application process. For the full programme listing, visit our Study in Malta page.

Key facts: Study in Malta 2026
  • EU membership: Malta joined the EU in 2004 — degrees recognised across all 27 member states
  • Official languages: Maltese and English — all accredited programmes taught in English
  • Bologna Process: MQF levels 1–8, directly mapped to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF)
  • Regulator: Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA) — licence required for all institutions
  • Sunny days: 300+ per year — one of Europe's sunniest countries
  • Safety: Consistently ranked in the EU's top 5 safest countries
  • Size: 316 km² — the entire island is smaller than most cities
  • University of Malta founded: 1592 — one of Europe's oldest universities

1. Malta's higher education system explained

Malta's higher education system is built on the British model and is fully aligned with the Bologna Process, the European framework that standardises degree structures and ensures mutual recognition across all EU member states. Every degree earned at a licensed Maltese institution carries the same structural recognition as a degree from France, Germany, or Italy.

The Maltese Qualifications Framework (MQF) governs all qualifications from level 1 (basic certificates) to level 8 (doctoral degrees) and maps directly to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF). This gives Maltese qualifications genuine and verifiable international weight.

The regulatory body is the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA), which licenses and quality-assures all further and higher education institutions operating in Malta. Before enrolling at any institution, verify its current MFHEA licence at mfhea.mt — a valid licence is mandatory for your student residence permit to be issued.

Qualification types available in Malta:

  • Certificate / Diploma — MQF levels 1–5, typically 1–2 years
  • Bachelor's Degree — MQF level 6, typically 3–4 years
  • Master's Degree — MQF level 7, typically 1–2 years
  • Doctoral Degree (PhD) — MQF level 8, typically 3–4 years
  • English Language Courses — from 2 weeks to 12 months, all CEFR levels (A1–C2)
2. Top universities and colleges in Malta in 2026

University of Malta (UM)
Founded in 1592, the University of Malta is the oldest and most prestigious institution on the island. It offers over 200 programmes across 14 faculties covering arts, sciences, engineering, law, medicine, economics, ICT, education, and more. The main campus is in Msida; a second campus operates in Gozo. The University is a member of the European University Association (EUA) and the Coimbra Group — both markers of academic standing.

MCAST — Malta College of Arts, Science and Technology
Malta's main vocational and technical institution, MCAST offers applied diplomas, higher national diplomas, and bachelor's-level qualifications across six institutes: Engineering, ICT, Business, Creative Arts, Health, and Maritime Studies. MCAST is popular with students who prefer hands-on, industry-focused education over purely academic programmes.

MFHEA-licensed private colleges (selection)

  • GGFL — Finance, law, and business at diploma and bachelor's level
  • St. Martin's Institute — ICT and business, affiliated with international universities
  • IDEA Academy — Design, creative arts, and media
  • AeSMI — Aviation and maritime, professional certifications
  • Cambridge School of Visual and Performing Arts — arts certificates and diplomas

Always verify: Check MFHEA licensing at mfhea.mt before enrolling. An unlicensed institution cannot support a valid student residence permit application — this is a hard requirement with no exceptions.

4. Malta student visa requirements 2026

EU / EEA / Swiss citizens
No student visa required. You can enrol in any accredited Maltese institution and reside freely under EU freedom of movement rules. Register your residence with Identità (Malta's identity management agency) within three months of arrival.

Non-EU citizens — Malta Long Stay Visa (Type D)
Non-EU students must apply for a Type D long-stay visa combined with the student residence permit. Applications are submitted through the Maltese embassy or consulate in your home country, or through Identità in Malta after arrival for certain nationalities.

Documents required for the Malta student visa 2026:

  1. Valid passport — minimum 6 months validity beyond your intended study period
  2. Completed visa and residence permit application form (Identità portal)
  3. Official letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution
  4. Proof of financial means — minimum €7,000 per year (bank statements, sponsor letter, or scholarship award letter)
  5. Proof of accommodation in Malta — rental agreement or student residence confirmation letter
  6. Comprehensive health insurance covering the full duration of studies
  7. Clean criminal record certificate — apostilled (from all countries of residence in the past 5 years)
  8. Academic transcripts and diplomas — apostilled or notarised and officially translated if not in English
  9. Passport-size photographs (biometric format)
  10. Visa and permit fee payment

Processing times:

  • Maltese embassy abroad: 4–8 weeks
  • Identità in Malta (in-country): 3–6 weeks
  • Expedited service (where available): 2–3 weeks

2026 update: Identità has expanded its e-services portal — many document submissions and biometric appointments can now be booked online. Check with your institution's international office for the latest process at the time of your application.

5. Student residence permit in Malta

For programmes lasting more than 90 days, non-EU students must hold a student residence permit issued by Identità. This permit is tied to your specific institution and must be renewed annually (or aligned with your academic calendar).

How to apply for the student residence permit:

  1. Receive your official acceptance letter from a licensed Maltese institution
  2. Gather all required documents (listed above under visa requirements)
  3. Submit application via the Identità online portal or at an Identità service centre
  4. Attend biometric appointment — fingerprints and photograph
  5. Receive your residence permit card, typically valid for 12 months
  6. Renew annually before expiry as long as you remain enrolled

Permit conditions to follow:

  • You must remain enrolled at the institution named on your permit
  • Notify Identità immediately if you change institution — failure to do so can invalidate your permit
  • Late renewal creates unlawful stay status even if your course is ongoing — renew at least 30 days before expiry
  • The permit is not automatically a work permit — working rights are granted separately within the permit conditions

6. Cost of studying in Malta in 2026

Tuition fees — international students:

  • University of Malta undergraduate: €3,000–€7,500/year
  • University of Malta postgraduate (Master's): €5,000–€12,000/year
  • MCAST diploma/HND: €2,500–€5,000/year
  • Private college undergraduate: €4,000–€10,000/year
  • English language school: €150–€350/week
  • MBA (private providers): €8,000–€15,000 total

Note: EU students at the University of Malta qualify for subsidised domestic tuition rates. Non-EU students pay the international fee band. Always confirm fees directly with your institution — prices shown are estimates.

Monthly living costs in Malta 2026:

  • Accommodation — shared apartment: €350–€600/month
  • Accommodation — student residence: €450–€750/month
  • Food and groceries: €150–€250/month
  • Public transport (student monthly pass): €26/month
  • Utilities (if not included): €50–€100/month
  • Mobile phone and internet: €20–€40/month
  • Eating out and social: €80–€200/month
  • Health insurance: €30–€80/month
  • Total estimate: €750–€1,100/month

How Malta compares to other EU study destinations:

  • Malta: €750–€1,100/month living costs, €3,000–€12,000/year tuition
  • UK (London): €2,000–€2,500/month, €15,000–€38,000/year tuition
  • Netherlands: €1,200–€1,600/month, €8,000–€15,000/year tuition
  • Germany: €900–€1,300/month, €500–€3,000/year tuition (semester fees)
  • Ireland: €1,400–€1,800/month, €9,000–€25,000/year tuition

Malta offers a significantly lower cost of living than all comparable Western European destinations while remaining inside the European Union — a key advantage for international students who need EU recognition and post-study work rights.

7. Scholarships for international students in Malta 2026

Funding your studies in Malta is more achievable than most students expect. Several national, EU-level, and institutional scholarship programmes exist specifically for international students.

Erasmus+ (EU students)
The Erasmus+ programme supports EU students studying abroad within Europe. If enrolled at a university in another EU country, a semester or full year at the University of Malta through an Erasmus+ exchange can attract a grant of €200–€800 per month, depending on your home country's cost-of-living tier.

Malta Government Scholarship Scheme (MGSS)
The Malta Government Scholarship Scheme, administered by Scholarships Malta, provides funding for both Maltese nationals and international students from countries with bilateral agreements. Categories include full scholarships, partial grants, and postdoctoral research fellowships. Annual deadline: February–March.

University of Malta bursaries
The University of Malta offers merit-based and needs-based bursaries through its Student Services department. International students enrolled in accredited programmes may apply. Awards typically reduce tuition by 10–30%, applied directly to your tuition invoice.

Commonwealth Scholarships
Malta is a Commonwealth member. The Commonwealth Scholarship and Fellowship Plan (CSFP) offers full tuition and living stipends for postgraduate study to students from Commonwealth developing nations. Deadline: November–December for the following academic year.

Institutional scholarships
Many MFHEA-licensed private colleges offer early-application discounts, merit awards, or regional scholarships. These vary by institution — always ask your admissions contact directly at the time of application.

8. Student accommodation in Malta

Malta's rental market is active — start your accommodation search at least 8–10 weeks before your course start date, especially if beginning in September or October when competition is highest.

Types of accommodation:

  • University Residence (Lija) — En-suite rooms managed by the University of Malta. Apply through the University's Student Services portal early; places fill by July for October intake.
  • Shared apartment — Most common option. Popular areas: Msida, Gzira, Sliema, St. Julian's, Birkirkara. Cost: €350–€600/month per room, typically furnished with bills.
  • Homestay — Room and meals with a Maltese family. Cost: €500–€850/month. Popular with English language students for cultural immersion.
  • Private student villages — Purpose-built student accommodation with Wi-Fi, gym, study spaces, and social areas. Cost: €550–€900/month. Higher cost but more amenities included.

Pro tip: Areas closest to the University of Malta (Msida, Gzira, Pietà) command a slight premium but save significant time and transport costs. Birkirkara and Ħamrun offer budget-friendly alternatives with good bus connections.

9. Working while studying in Malta in 2026

EU / EEA students: Full right to work — no hours cap, no permit required. You can work part-time, full-time, or freelance alongside your studies.

Non-EU students: Students on a valid residence permit may work up to 20 hours per week during term time. Full-time work is generally permitted during official university holidays and summer. Your employer must notify Jobsplus (Malta's employment authority) before you begin work.

Popular student jobs in Malta in 2026:

  • Hospitality — hotel reception, restaurant and bar service, tourism guides
  • Call centres and customer support (multi-language roles are highly sought after)
  • Retail — shop assistants, cashiers, stock management
  • Events — promotions, brand ambassadors, conference and trade show support
  • ICT support roles — for IT students with technical skills
  • English teaching assistance — for native English speakers

Minimum wage in Malta in 2026: €812.08/month (standard 40-hour week). Hourly equivalent: approximately €4.68/hour. Many hospitality and call centre roles pay above minimum wage, particularly for multilingual candidates.

10. Post-study work opportunities in Malta

One of Malta's most compelling advantages is what comes after graduation. The Maltese economy has been one of the fastest-growing in the EU for the past decade, driven by iGaming, fintech, financial services, ICT, and tourism.

EU graduates can remain and work in Malta without restriction. No permit conversion needed — EU freedom of movement applies indefinitely.

Non-EU graduates who completed a degree at a Maltese institution can apply to convert their student permit to a work permit through Jobsplus. This requires a job offer from a Maltese employer but Malta's labour market is active and regularly recruits from the international graduate pool — particularly in iGaming, fintech, ICT, and financial compliance roles.

Key industries hiring graduates in Malta 2026:

  • iGaming and online betting — Analyst, Marketing, Compliance, Customer Support: €28,000–€42,000/year
  • Financial services and fintech — Accountant, AML Analyst, Compliance Officer: €26,000–€40,000/year
  • ICT and software development — Developer, Data Analyst, IT Support: €28,000–€50,000/year
  • Tourism and hospitality — Hotel Management, Events Manager, Revenue Analyst: €22,000–€32,000/year
  • Healthcare — Nurse, Pharmacist, Allied Health Professional: €24,000–€38,000/year
11. English language courses in Malta

Malta is one of the world's premier English as a Foreign Language (EFL) destinations. With English as an official language used daily in shops, offices, media, and government, students are immersed in genuine English usage every minute of their stay — not just in the classroom.

Why Malta for English courses?

  • English spoken everywhere in daily life — genuine immersion environment
  • 30+ EAQUALS and FELTOM-accredited English language schools
  • Mediterranean climate — swim from May to November
  • Lower cost than UK, Ireland, Canada, or Australia
  • Easy travel base — flights to most European cities under 3 hours
  • Safe, compact island — easy to navigate as a newcomer

English course types available:

  • General English — A1 to C2, 15–30 lessons/week
  • IELTS and Cambridge exam preparation — B2 First, C1 Advanced, C2 Proficiency
  • Business English — professional communication and presentations
  • Academic English — university preparation (pre-sessional courses)
  • Intensive summer programmes — 2–8 weeks, popular with students aged 16–25
  • Long-term programmes — 3–12 months, leading to level certificates

Average English course costs in Malta 2026:

  • Standard General English (20 lessons/week) — 2 weeks: €300–€450
  • Intensive English (30 lessons/week) — 4 weeks: €700–€950
  • IELTS preparation — 4 weeks: €600–€900
  • Long-term programme — 12 weeks: €2,000–€3,500
12. Student life in Malta

Climate: Malta enjoys over 300 days of sunshine per year. Summers are hot (30–35°C), winters are mild (12–18°C). Students can realistically swim in the sea from May to November — a significant quality-of-life advantage over northern European study destinations.

Safety: Malta consistently ranks among the safest EU countries. Violent crime rates are extremely low. Solo students, including solo female students, consistently report feeling secure travelling and living on the island.

Transport: Malta Public Transport operates a comprehensive bus network. A student monthly pass costs approximately €26 — one of the cheapest public transport deals in Europe. The whole island is 27km long; anywhere can be reached in 30–45 minutes by bus.

Culture and social life: Valletta (the capital, and a UNESCO World Heritage City) offers world-class museums, baroque architecture, and a thriving restaurant and café scene. St. Julian's — specifically the Paceville neighbourhood — is Malta's nightlife hub, with bars, clubs, and restaurants popular with students and young professionals. Sliema's seafront is ideal for running, cycling, and socialising.

Sports and outdoor activities: Diving and snorkelling (Malta has crystal-clear waters and several Blue Flag beaches), hiking, water polo, football, sailing, and cycling are all popular. The University of Malta has sports facilities and 200+ student clubs and societies.

Weekend trips: Gozo (Malta's sister island, accessible by ferry in 25 minutes) is a popular weekend escape for hiking, diving, and a slower pace of life. Ferry routes also connect Malta to Sicily and southern Italy.

13. How to apply to study in Malta — step-by-step 2026
  1. Choose your institution and programme — Research MFHEA-licensed institutions and choose a programme matching your academic background and career goals. Browse all available programmes on our updated 2026 listing.
  2. Check entry requirements — Confirm the academic qualifications required. English proficiency: IELTS 5.5–6.0 for undergraduate, 6.0–6.5 for postgraduate (or equivalent Cambridge, TOEFL, or Duolingo score). Subject-specific prerequisites vary by programme.
  3. Submit your application — Apply directly through the institution's admissions portal, or through an authorised representative such as Outreach Recruitment. Supporting documents typically needed at this stage: transcripts, personal statement, references.
  4. Receive your letter of acceptance (LOA) — This official document is the cornerstone of your visa application. It must be on the institution's letterhead and confirm your programme, start date, and tuition amount.
  5. Apply for your visa and residence permit — Gather all required documents and submit through the Maltese embassy in your country or via the Identità online portal. Start this process at least 8 weeks before your course date.
  6. Arrange accommodation — Book accommodation before arriving. Start searching 10 weeks out if possible, especially for September intake.
  7. Arrive and register — Complete your institutional enrolment. Register your address with Identità within the required timeframe.
  8. Biometric appointment — Attend your permit biometrics appointment at Identità and collect your residence permit card when ready.
  9. Begin your studies — Attend orientation, access student support services, open a Maltese bank account, and explore the island.
14. Common mistakes international students make when applying to Malta
  1. Enrolling at an unlicensed institution — Always verify MFHEA licensing. An unlicensed institution cannot support a valid permit application — no exceptions.
  2. Applying for the visa too late — Visa and permit processing takes 4–8 weeks minimum. Many students apply 2–3 weeks before their course starts and miss their intake.
  3. Underestimating upfront costs — Budget for the first month in full before you leave: deposit (typically 1–2 months' rent), first month's rent, health insurance premium, and 4 weeks of living expenses before your first income.
  4. Not checking permit conditions before working — Working above your permit's hours limit (20 hrs/week for non-EU) can invalidate your permit and affect future visa applications.
  5. Missing residence permit renewal deadlines — Set a reminder 60 days before your permit expires. Late renewal creates unlawful stay status even if your course is running normally.
  6. Assuming English course students automatically qualify — Short English courses (under 15 hrs/week) may not meet the minimum intensity threshold for student permit eligibility. Confirm with your school before booking.
  7. Going it alone without a qualified advisor — Navigating MFHEA requirements, Identità processes, and institutional applications simultaneously is complex. Using an authorised advisor reduces errors, delays, and refusals significantly.
15. Malta vs other study destinations: honest comparison
  • Malta vs UK: Malta is significantly cheaper (tuition and living). Degrees are EU-recognised, not UK-recognised post-Brexit. Malta is better for those who want EU freedom of movement post-study. UK is better for English-speaking job markets in the US, Canada, and Australia.
  • Malta vs Germany: Germany offers near-free tuition but requires German language proficiency for most programmes. Malta teaches everything in English. Malta is better for anglophone students; Germany for those committed to learning German.
  • Malta vs Netherlands: Similar English-language offering, but Dutch tuition and living costs are 40–60% higher. Malta's iGaming and fintech sector competes well with Amsterdam's tech scene for specific profiles.
  • Malta vs Cyprus: Both are small EU islands with English as a working language. Malta has more established English language schools and a more diverse economy. Cyprus has a larger international student community and some private universities with US affiliations.

16. Frequently asked questions — Study in Malta 2026

Can international students study in Malta?
Yes. Malta welcomes international students from all countries. EU students need no visa. Non-EU students require a student visa and residence permit from Identità. All accredited programmes at licensed institutions are taught in English.

What English level do I need to study in Malta?
Most undergraduate programmes require IELTS 5.5–6.0 (or equivalent). Master's programmes require 6.0–6.5. Some institutions accept a previous degree taught in English as proof of proficiency in lieu of a formal test.

How long does the Malta student permit take?
Processing takes 3–8 weeks depending on your application route. Apply at minimum 8 weeks before your course start date. Embassy applications take longer than in-country Identità applications.

Are Maltese degrees recognised internationally?
Yes. Malta follows the Bologna Process. Degrees from the University of Malta and licensed institutions are recognised across all 27 EU member states. Non-EU recognition depends on bilateral agreements in your destination country but is generally strong, particularly for UM degrees.

Can I stay in Malta after graduating?
EU graduates can remain and work without restriction. Non-EU graduates need a job offer to convert their student permit to a work permit through Jobsplus. Malta's growing economy — especially iGaming, fintech, and ICT — regularly recruits international graduates.

Is Malta safe for international students?
Yes. Malta consistently ranks among the EU's top 5 safest countries. Very low violent crime rates. Solo female students and young international students report feeling very safe across the island.

What scholarships are available in Malta?
Erasmus+ (EU students), Malta Government Scholarship Scheme, University of Malta bursaries, Commonwealth Scholarships, and institutional awards. Deadlines fall between November and March for the following academic year. See our full scholarships guide at Malta Study Guide.

Do I need an IELTS to study in Malta?
Most institutions require IELTS 5.5–6.5 depending on level. Alternatives include Cambridge B2 First, TOEFL iBT 72+, Duolingo English Test, or a previous qualification taught in English. Confirm with your specific institution.

  • Malta is a full EU member state — degrees recognised across all 27 EU countries

  • Non-EU students need a student visa (Type D) and residence permit from Identità
  • Tuition: €3,000–€12,000/year; living costs: €750–€1,100/month

Conclusion — Is Malta the right study destination for you in 2026?

Malta in 2026 is a study destination that punches well above its weight. A fully EU-recognised degree, English as an official language, Mediterranean quality of life, affordable costs, and an active job market — few places in Europe offer this combination simultaneously.

The students who make it to Malta in 2026 are the ones who start their applications now. Visa processing takes time. Accommodation fills fast. Scholarship deadlines pass quickly. The preparation window matters.
