Studying in Malta gives international students access to EU-recognised degrees taught in English at some of the Mediterranean’s most affordable institutions. Students can enrol in bachelor’s, master’s, or language programmes and apply for a student residence permit valid for the duration of their course.

Key Takeaways

  • Malta offers English-taught, EU-recognised study routes through the University of Malta, MCAST, and MFHEA-licensed private colleges.
  • Non-EU students usually need a Type D visa and a student residence permit; EU/EEA/Swiss students do not need a student visa.
  • Typical international tuition ranges from about €3,000 to €12,000 per year, depending on institution and programme level.
  • Students should prepare acceptance, funds, accommodation, insurance, and apostilled academic/criminal record documents before applying.
  • Malta is strongest for students targeting business, ICT, finance, hospitality, maritime, aviation, and English language pathways.

1. Malta’s higher education system explained

Malta’s higher education system is built on the British academic model and fully aligned with the Bologna Process, the European framework that standardises degree structures and ensures mutual recognition across all EU member states.

The Maltese Qualifications Framework (MQF) governs all qualifications from level 1 to level 8 and maps directly to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF). Every degree earned at a licensed Maltese institution carries verifiable EU recognition.

The regulatory body is the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA). A valid MFHEA licence is mandatory for your student residence permit to be issued — always verify at mfhea.mt.

  • Certificate / Diploma — MQF 1–5, typically 1–2 years
  • Bachelor’s Degree — MQF 6, typically 3–4 years
  • Master’s Degree — MQF 7, typically 1–2 years
  • Doctoral Degree (PhD) — MQF 8, typically 3–4 years
  • English Language Courses — 2 weeks to 12 months, all CEFR levels
2. Top universities and colleges in Malta

Malta’s main public university was founded in 1592 and is a member of the European University Association. International students choose from one public university, one vocational college, and a range of MFHEA-licensed private institutions — all teaching in English.

University of Malta (UM): Founded in 1592. 200+ programmes across 14 faculties. Main campus in Msida; second campus in Gozo. Member of the EUA and the Coimbra Group.

MCAST: Six institutes: Engineering, ICT, Business, Creative Arts, Health, and Maritime Studies. Popular with students preferring applied, industry-focused learning.

MFHEA-licensed private colleges (selection):

  • GGFL — Finance, law, business (diploma and bachelor’s)
  • St. Martin’s Institute — ICT and business
  • IDEA Academy — Design, creative arts, and media
  • AeSMI — Aviation and maritime professional certifications

4. Malta student visa requirements 2026

EU and EEA citizens need no visa to study in Malta. Non-EU students must apply for a Malta Long Stay Visa (Type D) combined with a student residence permit before or shortly after arriving.

EU / EEA / Swiss citizens: No student visa required. Register with Identità within three months of arrival.

Non-EU students — documents required:

  1. Valid passport — minimum 6 months validity beyond your study period
  2. Completed application form via the Identità e-services portal
  3. Official letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution
  4. Proof of financial means — minimum €7,000 per year
  5. Proof of accommodation — rental agreement or student residence confirmation
  6. Comprehensive health insurance for the full study period
  7. Clean criminal record certificate — apostilled, covering all countries of residence in the past 5 years
  8. Academic transcripts and diplomas — apostilled or notarised; translated if not in English
  9. Passport-size photographs (biometric format)
  10. Visa and permit fee payment

Processing times: Maltese embassy abroad: 4–8 weeks. Identità in Malta: 3–6 weeks. Apply at least 8 weeks before your course start date.

2026 note: Identità has expanded its e-services portal — biometric appointments can now be booked online.

Which student route applies to you?

You can enter Malta without a student visa. If your stay is longer than three months, register your residence and keep proof of enrolment, address, health coverage, and financial means.

Focus first on admission deadlines, accommodation, and programme confirmation before travel.

You should plan the Type D visa and student residence permit early. Prepare acceptance, accommodation, funds, insurance, passport, academic records, and apostilled criminal record documents before submission.

Apply at least 8 weeks before your course start date, and avoid paying non-refundable travel costs before your visa route is clear.

5. Student residence permit in Malta

Non-EU students enrolled in programmes lasting more than 90 days must hold a student residence permit issued by Identità. The permit is tied to your enrolled institution and renewed annually.

  • Submit application via the Identità portal or at a service centre
  • Attend biometric appointment — fingerprints and photograph
  • Permit card typically valid for 12 months; renew before expiry
  • Notify Identità immediately if you change institution
  • Late renewal creates unlawful stay status — renew at least 30 days before expiry

6. Cost of studying in Malta in 2026

Malta’s tuition fees are significantly lower than comparable EU study destinations, and living costs average €750–€1,100 per month — well below London, Amsterdam, or Dublin.

Tuition fees — international students:

  • University of Malta undergraduate: €3,000–€7,500/year
  • University of Malta postgraduate: €5,000–€12,000/year
  • MCAST diploma/HND: €2,500–€5,000/year
  • Private college undergraduate: €4,000–€10,000/year
  • English language school: €150–€350/week

Monthly living costs in Malta 2026:

  • Accommodation (shared apartment): €350–€600
  • Accommodation (student residence): €450–€750
  • Food and groceries: €150–€250
  • Public transport (student monthly pass): €26
  • Utilities (if not included): €50–€100
  • Mobile and internet: €20–€40
  • Eating out and social: €80–€200
  • Health insurance: €30–€80
  • Total estimate: €750–€1,100/month
Malta vs other EU study destinations — cost comparison 2026
  • Malta: €750–€1,100/month living | €3,000–€12,000/year tuition
  • UK (London): €2,000–€2,500/month | €15,000–€38,000/year
  • Netherlands: €1,200–€1,600/month | €8,000–€15,000/year
  • Ireland: €1,400–€1,800/month | €9,000–€25,000/year
  • Germany: €900–€1,300/month | €500–€3,000/year (semester fees)

7. Scholarships for international students in Malta 2026

Several national, EU-level, and institutional scholarship programmes exist specifically for international students in Malta, ranging from full tuition coverage to partial grants.

  • Erasmus+ — EU students on exchange; €200–€800/month; apply through your home university
  • Malta Government Scholarship Scheme (MGSS) — full and partial grants; deadline February–March annually; see scholarshipsmalta.gov.mt
  • University of Malta bursaries — merit and needs-based; 10–30% tuition reduction
  • Commonwealth Scholarships — full tuition and stipend; deadline November–December
  • Institutional awards — early-application discounts at private colleges
8. Student accommodation in Malta

Finding accommodation in Malta is manageable but the market moves quickly. Start your search at least 8–10 weeks before your course starts, especially for September intake.

  • University Residence (Lija) — En-suite rooms managed by UM; apply early; fills by July for October intake
  • Shared apartment — Most common; popular areas: Msida, Gzira, Sliema, St. Julian’s; €350–€600/month
  • Homestay — Room and meals with a Maltese family; €500–€850/month; ideal for EFL students
  • Private student villages — Purpose-built; Wi-Fi, gym, study spaces; €550–€900/month
9. Working while studying in Malta in 2026

EU students have no work restrictions in Malta. Non-EU students on a valid student residence permit may work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official academic holidays.

  • EU/EEA students — unlimited right to work; no permit required
  • Non-EU students — up to 20 hrs/week during term; full-time in official holidays; employer must notify Jobsplus
  • Minimum wage 2026 — €812.08/month (standard 40-hour week)
  • Popular student jobs — hospitality, call centres, retail, events, ICT support
10. Post-study work opportunities in Malta

Malta’s economy is one of the EU’s fastest-growing, driven by iGaming, fintech, financial services, and ICT. EU graduates can remain and work freely. Non-EU graduates can convert their student permit to a work permit through a job offer via Jobsplus.

  • iGaming and online betting — €28,000–€42,000/year
  • Financial services and fintech — €26,000–€40,000/year
  • ICT and software — €28,000–€50,000/year
  • Tourism and hospitality — €22,000–€32,000/year
  • Healthcare — €24,000–€38,000/year
11. English language courses in Malta

Malta is a world-leading EFL destination. With English as an official language used daily in shops, offices, and media, students experience genuine immersion outside the classroom.

  • 30+ EAQUALS/FELTOM-accredited schools — quality guaranteed
  • Course types — General English (A1–C2), IELTS/Cambridge prep, Business English, Academic English
  • Cost — €150–€350/week (general); €600–€900 for 4-week exam prep
12. Student life in Malta

Living in Malta as a student means Mediterranean weather, a compact island, a safe environment, and a rich cultural life — all while studying towards an EU-recognised degree.

  • Climate — 300+ sunny days/year; swimming season May–November
  • Safety — consistently top 5 safest EU countries
  • Transport — student monthly bus pass €26; entire island reachable in 30–45 minutes
  • Culture — Valletta is a UNESCO World Heritage City; St. Julian’s is the nightlife hub; Gozo (ferry, 25 min) is a weekend escape
  • Student community — University of Malta has 200+ clubs and societies; strong international network

13. How to apply to study in Malta — step-by-step guide

Applying to study in Malta involves nine steps, from choosing your institution to collecting your residence permit card. Start at least three months before your intended course date.

  1. Choose your institution and programme — Research MFHEA-licensed institutions. View all available programmes on our 2026 listing.
  2. Check entry requirements — IELTS 5.5–6.0 for undergraduate; 6.0–6.5 for postgraduate.
  3. Submit your application — Apply through the institution’s admissions portal or via an authorised representative such as Outreach Recruitment.
  4. Receive your letter of acceptance (LOA) — Confirms your programme, start date, and tuition amount. Cornerstone document for your visa application.
  5. Apply for your visa and residence permit — Gather all required documents. Apply at least 8 weeks before your course date.
  6. Arrange accommodation — Book before arriving. Start searching 10 weeks out, especially for September intake.
  7. Arrive and complete enrolment — Complete institutional enrolment and register your address with Identità.
  8. Attend biometric appointment — Book via the Identità e-services portal.
  9. Collect your residence permit card — Collect from Identità and begin your studies.
14. Common mistakes international students make

The most preventable problems in Malta student applications come down to seven recurring mistakes. Avoid these and your path to a Maltese study permit will be significantly smoother.

  1. Enrolling at an unlicensed institution — Verify MFHEA licence at mfhea.mt first. No licence = no permit.
  2. Applying for the visa too late — Processing takes 4–8 weeks minimum.
  3. Underestimating upfront costs — Budget for deposit, first month’s rent, insurance, and 4 weeks of living expenses.
  4. Working above permit hours — Exceeding 20 hrs/week as a non-EU student can invalidate your permit.
  5. Missing renewal deadlines — Renew at least 30 days before expiry.
  6. Assuming all English courses qualify — Courses under 15 hrs/week may not meet permit eligibility.
  7. Not using a qualified advisor — An authorised advisor reduces errors and delays significantly.
15. Malta vs other study destinations — honest comparison
  • Malta vs UK — Malta is significantly cheaper. UK degrees carry stronger brand recognition outside the EU.
  • Malta vs Germany — Germany has near-free tuition but most programmes require German. Malta teaches entirely in English.
  • Malta vs Netherlands — Similar English-language offering but Dutch living costs are 40–60% higher.
  • Malta vs Cyprus — Both are small EU English-speaking islands. Malta has a more established EFL industry and a more diverse economy.
Frequently Asked Questions
Can international students study in Malta?

Yes. Malta welcomes international students from EU and non-EU countries. Non-EU students need a Type D student visa and a residence permit from Identità. All accredited programmes are taught in English, and degrees are recognised across all 27 EU member states.

How much does it cost to study in Malta in 2026?

Tuition fees at the University of Malta range from €3,000 to €7,500/year for undergraduates. Private colleges charge €4,000 to €10,000/year. Monthly living costs average €750 to €1,100, including accommodation, food, transport, and health insurance.

What documents are needed for the Malta student visa?

You need: a valid passport, letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution, proof of financial means (€7,000+/year), proof of accommodation, comprehensive health insurance, a clean criminal record certificate (apostilled), and academic transcripts. Processing takes 4–8 weeks.

Can I work while studying in Malta?

EU students have no work restrictions. Non-EU students on a valid student residence permit may work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official academic holidays. Employers must notify Jobsplus. Minimum wage in 2026 is €812.08/month.

Are degrees from Malta recognised internationally?

Yes. Malta follows the Bologna Process and the Maltese Qualifications Framework maps directly to the European Qualifications Framework. University of Malta degrees are recognised across all 27 EU member states and widely accepted by employers globally.

What scholarships are available to international students in Malta?

Key scholarships include: Erasmus+ (€200–€800/month for EU students), the Malta Government Scholarship Scheme, University of Malta bursaries (10–30% tuition reduction), and Commonwealth Scholarships. Deadlines fall between November and March annually.

Is Malta in the Schengen Area?

Yes. Malta joined the Schengen Area in 2007. A student residence permit allows travel within the Schengen zone for short stays — up to 90 days in any 180-day period in other Schengen countries — while you are enrolled in Malta.

Key takeaways
  • Malta is a full EU member state — degrees recognised across all 27 EU countries
  • English is an official language — no language barrier for international students
  • Non-EU students need a student visa (Type D) and residence permit from Identità
  • Tuition: €3,000–€12,000/year; living costs: €750–€1,100/month
  • Non-EU students can work up to 20 hrs/week during term time
  • Malta is in the Schengen Area — your permit enables free travel across Schengen Europe
  • Always verify MFHEA licensing before enrolling
  • Apply at least 8 weeks before your course start date
Conclusion — is Malta the right study destination for you in 2026?

Malta in 2026 is a study destination that punches well above its weight. A fully EU-recognised degree, English as an official language, Mediterranean quality of life, affordable costs, and an active post-graduation job market — few places in Europe offer this combination simultaneously.
