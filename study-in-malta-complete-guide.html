Why Malta in 2026?

Malta is a full EU member state where all accredited programmes are taught in English — one of only two EU countries with English as an official language. It combines EU-recognised degrees, affordable tuition fees, Mediterranean quality of life, and a growing job market in iGaming, fintech, and ICT, making it one of Europe's strongest study destinations for 2026.

  • EU membership since 2004 — degrees recognised across all 27 member states
  • English official language — genuine immersion, no language barrier
  • Bologna Process aligned — MQF levels 1–8 map directly to the EQF
  • Regulator: MFHEA — verify licensing at mfhea.mt before enrolling
  • 300+ sunny days/year — one of Europe's sunniest countries
  • Safe — consistently top 5 in EU safety rankings
1. Malta's higher education system explained

Malta's higher education system is built on the British academic model and fully aligned with the Bologna Process, the European framework that standardises degree structures and ensures mutual recognition across all EU member states.

The Maltese Qualifications Framework (MQF) governs all qualifications from level 1 to level 8 and maps directly to the European Qualifications Framework (EQF). Every degree earned at a licensed Maltese institution carries verifiable EU recognition.

The regulatory body is the Malta Further and Higher Education Authority (MFHEA). A valid MFHEA licence is mandatory for your student residence permit to be issued — always verify at mfhea.mt.

  • Certificate / Diploma — MQF 1–5, typically 1–2 years
  • Bachelor's Degree — MQF 6, typically 3–4 years
  • Master's Degree — MQF 7, typically 1–2 years
  • Doctoral Degree (PhD) — MQF 8, typically 3–4 years
  • English Language Courses — 2 weeks to 12 months, all CEFR levels
2. Top universities and colleges in Malta

Malta's main public university was founded in 1592 and is a member of the European University Association. International students choose from one public university, one vocational college, and a range of MFHEA-licensed private institutions — all teaching in English.

University of Malta (UM): Founded in 1592. 200+ programmes across 14 faculties — arts, sciences, engineering, law, medicine, economics, ICT, and more. Main campus in Msida; second campus in Gozo. Member of the European University Association (EUA) and the Coimbra Group.

MCAST — Malta College of Arts, Science and Technology: Vocational and technical education. Six institutes: Engineering, ICT, Business, Creative Arts, Health, and Maritime Studies. Popular with students preferring applied, industry-focused learning.

MFHEA-licensed private colleges (selection):

  • GGFL — Finance, law, business (diploma and bachelor's)
  • St. Martin's Institute — ICT and business, affiliated with international universities
  • IDEA Academy — Design, creative arts, and media
  • AeSMI — Aviation and maritime professional certifications

3. Study programmes available in Malta

Malta offers English-taught programmes across all major disciplines. The most in-demand fields for international students in 2026 are business, ICT, law, finance, health sciences, and maritime studies.

  • Business & Management — 3 years Bachelor's; UM, MCAST, private colleges
  • Information Technology & AI — 3 years Bachelor's; strong employer demand in Malta
  • Law & Legal Studies — 4 years Bachelor's; EU law focus at UM and GGFL
  • Engineering & Technology — 4 years Bachelor's; UM and MCAST; good placement record
  • Health Sciences & Medicine — 5–6 years MD; UM only; competitive entry
  • Finance & Economics — 3 years Bachelor's; UM and GGFL; Malta's financial sector is a key employer
  • Maritime & Aviation — 1–2 years Diploma; MCAST and AeSMI; industry certifications
  • English Language Courses — 2 weeks to 12 months; 30+ EAQUALS/FELTOM-accredited schools
  • MBA & Master's — 1–2 years; UM and private providers; part-time options available
4. Malta student visa requirements 2026

EU and EEA citizens need no visa to study in Malta. Non-EU students must apply for a Malta Long Stay Visa (Type D) combined with a student residence permit before or shortly after arriving in Malta.

EU / EEA / Swiss citizens: No student visa required. Register with Identità within three months of arrival.

Non-EU students — documents required:

  1. Valid passport — minimum 6 months validity beyond your study period
  2. Completed application form via the Identità e-services portal
  3. Official letter of acceptance from an MFHEA-licensed institution
  4. Proof of financial means — minimum €7,000 per year (bank statements, sponsor letter, or scholarship award)
  5. Proof of accommodation — rental agreement or student residence confirmation
  6. Comprehensive health insurance for the full study period
  7. Clean criminal record certificate — apostilled, covering all countries of residence in the past 5 years
  8. Academic transcripts and diplomas — apostilled or notarised; officially translated if not in English
  9. Passport-size photographs (biometric format)
  10. Visa and permit fee payment

Processing times: Maltese embassy abroad: 4–8 weeks. Identità in Malta: 3–6 weeks. Apply at least 8 weeks before your course start date.

2026 note: Identità has expanded its e-services portal — biometric appointments can now be booked online. Check with your institution's international office for the latest process.

5. Student residence permit in Malta

Non-EU students enrolled in programmes lasting more than 90 days must hold a student residence permit issued by Identità. The permit is tied to your enrolled institution and must be renewed annually.

  • Submit application via the Identità portal or at an Identità service centre
  • Attend biometric appointment — fingerprints and photograph
  • Permit card typically valid for 12 months; renew before expiry
  • Notify Identità immediately if you change institution
  • Late renewal creates unlawful stay status — renew at least 30 days before expiry

6. Cost of studying in Malta in 2026

Malta's tuition fees are significantly lower than comparable EU study destinations, and living costs average €750–€1,100 per month — well below London, Amsterdam, or Dublin.

Tuition fees — international students:

  • University of Malta undergraduate: €3,000–€7,500/year
  • University of Malta postgraduate: €5,000–€12,000/year
  • MCAST diploma/HND: €2,500–€5,000/year
  • Private college undergraduate: €4,000–€10,000/year
  • English language school: €150–€350/week

Monthly living costs in Malta 2026:

  • Accommodation (shared apartment): €350–€600
  • Accommodation (student residence): €450–€750
  • Food and groceries: €150–€250
  • Public transport (student monthly pass): €26
  • Utilities (if not included): €50–€100
  • Mobile and internet: €20–€40
  • Eating out and social: €80–€200
  • Health insurance: €30–€80
  • Total estimate: €750–€1,100/month
Malta vs other EU study destinations — cost comparison 2026
  • Malta: €750–€1,100/month living | €3,000–€12,000/year tuition
  • UK (London): €2,000–€2,500/month living | €15,000–€38,000/year tuition
  • Netherlands: €1,200–€1,600/month living | €8,000–€15,000/year tuition
  • Ireland: €1,400–€1,800/month living | €9,000–€25,000/year tuition
  • Germany: €900–€1,300/month living | €500–€3,000/year (semester fees)

7. Scholarships for international students in Malta 2026

Several national, EU-level, and institutional scholarship programmes exist specifically for international students in Malta, ranging from full tuition coverage to partial grants.

  • Erasmus+ — EU students on exchange; €200–€800/month; apply through your home university
  • Malta Government Scholarship Scheme (MGSS) — full and partial grants; deadline February–March annually; see scholarshipsmalta.gov.mt
  • University of Malta bursaries — merit and needs-based; 10–30% tuition reduction; apply through UM Student Services
  • Commonwealth Scholarships — full tuition and stipend for Commonwealth nationals at postgraduate level; deadline November–December
  • Institutional awards — early-application discounts and regional scholarships at private colleges; ask your admissions contact directly
8. Student accommodation in Malta

Finding accommodation in Malta is manageable but the market moves quickly. Start your search at least 8–10 weeks before your course starts, especially for September intake.

  • University Residence (Lija) — En-suite rooms managed by UM; apply through Student Services portal early; fills by July for October intake
  • Shared apartment — Most common option; popular areas: Msida, Gzira, Sliema, St. Julian's, Birkirkara; €350–€600/month including bills
  • Homestay — Room and meals with a Maltese family; €500–€850/month; ideal for English language students
  • Private student villages — Purpose-built; Wi-Fi, gym, study spaces; €550–€900/month; more amenities but higher cost
9. Working while studying in Malta in 2026

EU students have no work restrictions in Malta. Non-EU students on a valid student residence permit may work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during official academic holidays.

  • EU/EEA students — unlimited right to work; no permit required
  • Non-EU students — up to 20 hrs/week during term; full-time in official holidays; employer must notify Jobsplus
  • Minimum wage 2026 — €812.08/month (standard 40-hour week)
  • Popular student jobs — hospitality, call centres, retail, events, ICT support, English teaching assistance
10. Post-study work opportunities in Malta

Malta's economy is one of the EU's fastest-growing, driven by iGaming, fintech, financial services, and ICT. EU graduates can remain and work freely. Non-EU graduates can convert their student permit to a work permit through a job offer via Jobsplus.

  • iGaming and online betting — Analyst, Marketing, Compliance: €28,000–€42,000/year
  • Financial services and fintech — Accountant, AML, Compliance: €26,000–€40,000/year
  • ICT and software — Developer, Data Analyst, IT Support: €28,000–€50,000/year
  • Tourism and hospitality — Hotel Management, Events: €22,000–€32,000/year
  • Healthcare — Nurse, Pharmacist, Allied Health: €24,000–€38,000/year
11. English language courses in Malta

Malta is a world-leading EFL destination. With English as an official language used daily in shops, offices, and media, students experience genuine immersion outside the classroom every day.

  • 30+ EAQUALS/FELTOM-accredited schools — quality guaranteed
  • Course types — General English (A1–C2), IELTS/Cambridge exam preparation, Business English, Academic English, intensive summer programmes
  • Cost — €150–€350/week (general); €600–€900 for 4-week exam prep
  • Why Malta vs UK/Ireland — 40–60% lower cost, Mediterranean climate, genuine English immersion

12. Student life in Malta

Living in Malta as a student means Mediterranean weather, a compact and walkable island, a safe environment, and a rich cultural life — all while studying towards an EU-recognised degree.

  • Climate — 300+ sunny days/year; swimming season May–November; mild winters
  • Safety — consistently top 5 safest EU countries; very low violent crime rate
  • Transport — student monthly bus pass €26; entire island reachable in 30–45 minutes
  • Culture — Valletta is a UNESCO World Heritage City and European Capital of Culture; St. Julian's is the nightlife hub; Gozo (ferry, 25 min) is a popular weekend escape
  • Sports — diving, snorkelling, football, water polo, hiking, sailing
  • Student community — University of Malta has 200+ clubs and societies; strong international student network
13. How to apply to study in Malta — step-by-step guide

Applying to study in Malta involves nine steps, from choosing your institution to collecting your residence permit card. Start at least three months before your intended course date.

  1. Choose your institution and programme — Research MFHEA-licensed institutions. View all available programmes on our 2026 listing.
  2. Check entry requirements — IELTS 5.5–6.0 for undergraduate; 6.0–6.5 for postgraduate. Confirm subject prerequisites directly with the institution.
  3. Submit your application — Apply through the institution's admissions portal or via an authorised representative such as Outreach Recruitment.
  4. Receive your letter of acceptance (LOA) — The LOA confirms your programme, start date, and tuition amount. This is the cornerstone document for your visa application.
  5. Apply for your visa and residence permit — Gather all required documents and apply at least 8 weeks before your course date.
  6. Arrange accommodation — Book before arriving. Start searching 10 weeks out, especially for September intake.
  7. Arrive and complete enrolment — Complete institutional enrolment and register your address with Identità within the required timeframe.
  8. Attend biometric appointment — Book via the Identità e-services portal and attend your fingerprints/photo appointment.
  9. Collect your residence permit card — Collect from Identità and begin your studies.
14. Common mistakes international students make

The most preventable problems in Malta student applications come down to seven recurring mistakes. Avoid these and your path to a Maltese study permit will be significantly smoother.

  1. Enrolling at an unlicensed institution — Verify MFHEA licence at mfhea.mt before paying any fees. No licence = no permit.
  2. Applying for the visa too late — Processing takes 4–8 weeks minimum. Many students apply 2–3 weeks before their course and miss the intake.
  3. Underestimating upfront costs — Budget for: deposit (1–2 months' rent), first month's rent, insurance premium, and 4 weeks of living expenses before any income arrives.
  4. Working above permit hours — Exceeding 20 hrs/week as a non-EU student can invalidate your permit and affect future visa applications.
  5. Missing renewal deadlines — Set a reminder 60 days before permit expiry. Late renewal = unlawful stay status.
  6. Assuming all English courses qualify — Courses under 15 hrs/week may not meet the minimum intensity for permit eligibility. Confirm with your school first.
  7. Not using a qualified advisor — Navigating MFHEA, Identità, and institutional requirements simultaneously is complex. An authorised advisor reduces errors and delays significantly.
15. Malta vs other study destinations — honest comparison

Malta is not the right choice for every student, but for international students who want an EU degree, English instruction, and an affordable Mediterranean lifestyle, few destinations come close to its value proposition.

  • Malta vs UK — Malta is significantly cheaper. UK degrees carry stronger brand recognition in English-speaking markets outside the EU. Malta is better for EU freedom of movement post-study.
  • Malta vs Germany — Germany has near-free tuition but requires German language proficiency for most programmes. Malta teaches entirely in English. Malta wins for anglophone students.
  • Malta vs Netherlands — Similar English-language offering but Dutch living costs are 40–60% higher. Malta's iGaming and fintech sector competes well with Amsterdam's tech scene.
  • Malta vs Cyprus — Both are small EU English-speaking islands. Malta has a more established EFL industry and a more diverse economy. Cyprus has a larger international student community at certain private universities.
16. FAQs — study in Malta 2026

Can international students study in Malta? Yes. EU students need no visa. Non-EU students need a Type D visa and residence permit from Identità. All accredited programmes are taught in English.

What IELTS score do I need? Most undergraduate programmes require 5.5–6.0. Master's programmes typically require 6.0–6.5. Some institutions accept previous English-taught qualifications as proof of proficiency.

How long does the student permit take? 3–8 weeks. Apply at minimum 8 weeks before your course starts.

Are Maltese degrees internationally recognised? Yes. Malta follows the Bologna Process. Degrees are recognised across all 27 EU member states. UM is a member of the EUA and the Coimbra Group.

Can I stay in Malta after graduating? EU graduates can remain and work freely. Non-EU graduates need a job offer to convert their permit via Jobsplus. Malta's job market, particularly in iGaming, fintech, and ICT, actively recruits international graduates.

Is Malta in the Schengen Area? Yes. Malta joined the Schengen Area in 2007. A Malta student residence permit allows you to travel freely within the Schengen zone for short stays (up to 90 days in any 180-day period in other Schengen countries).

What scholarships are available? Erasmus+, Malta Government Scholarship Scheme, University of Malta bursaries, and Commonwealth Scholarships. Full details at our complete Malta Study Guide.

Key takeaways
  • Malta is a full EU member state — degrees recognised across all 27 EU countries
  • English is an official language — no language barrier for international students
  • Non-EU students need a student visa (Type D) and residence permit from Identità
  • Tuition: €3,000–€12,000/year; living costs: €750–€1,100/month
  • Non-EU students can work up to 20 hrs/week during term time
  • Malta is in the Schengen Area — your permit enables free travel across Schengen Europe
  • Always verify MFHEA licensing before enrolling — mandatory for permit eligibility
  • Apply for your permit at least 8 weeks before your course start date
Conclusion — is Malta the right study destination for you in 2026?

Malta in 2026 is a study destination that punches well above its weight. A fully EU-recognised degree, English as an official language, Mediterranean quality of life, affordable costs, and an active post-graduation job market — few places in Europe offer this combination simultaneously.

The students who make it to Malta in 2026 are the ones who start their applications now. Visa processing takes time. Accommodation fills fast. Scholarship deadlines pass quickly.
