How This Calculator Works

The calculator applies Malta's three tax computation methods — Single, Married, and Parent — each with its own progressive bands, plus a widened 0% band if you have one or two-or-more qualifying children. It then applies standard Class 1 social security contributions (10% of gross wage, with a floor and a weekly cap) to estimate your net pay.

Social Security Contributions (SSC) / National Insurance

Malta's Class 1 social security contribution is the equivalent of what's called National Insurance in other countries, and depends on age, student status, and birth year, not just income. Standard adult employees pay 10% of gross weekly wage, with a floor of €22.94 for lower earners and a cap of €54.43 for higher earners; the cap is €45.19 for employees born on or before 31 December 1961. Under-18 employees pay a fixed €6.62 a week. Employees on the student-worker scheme pay 10% of wage capped at €4.38 (under 18) or €7.94 (18 and over) a week. Use the checkboxes above the calculator to match your own circumstances.

FAQs

How accurate is this salary calculator?

It gives a close estimate based on Malta's standard 2026 tax bands and social security rates for a typical employee. It doesn't account for every personal circumstance, so treat the result as a guide rather than an exact payslip figure.

What's the difference between Single, Married, and Parent tax status?

These are Malta's three tax computation methods. Married and Parent rates have a wider 0% tax band than Single rates, and the band widens further if you have one or two-or-more qualifying children. Your actual eligibility depends on your personal circumstances — check with the Commissioner for Revenue if you're unsure which applies to you.

Is the statutory bonus included in my net salary?

No. The statutory government bonus is paid and taxed separately from your regular salary, four times a year. The calculator shows it as a separate reference figure rather than folding it into the net salary total.

How is social security (SSC) / National Insurance calculated in Malta?

Malta calls this Social Security Contributions (SSC) -- it's the same idea as National Insurance elsewhere. For most adult employees, it's 10% of gross weekly wage, with a minimum weekly contribution for lower earners and a maximum weekly cap for higher earners (a lower cap applies if you were born on or before 31 December 1961). Under-18s pay a fixed weekly amount, and students on the student-worker scheme pay a lower capped rate. The calculator's checkboxes let you match your own circumstances.
